01 / A connected set of water stores

EcoSIM advances liquid water and ice in two soil pore domains, while exchanging water and energy with snow, litter, the atmosphere, plant roots, neighboring cells, and external boundaries.

The standard EcoSIM driver calls HOUR1 before WATSUB. Hydraulic properties and forcing are prepared upstream; watsub coordinates a sequence of shorter water–heat steps. It is a staged flux-and-storage calculation, not a single global nonlinear Richards-equation solve.

02 / One hourly call, many short steps

Before the loop, LocalCopySoilVars copies persistent states into working arrays, calculates pore air volumes and heat capacities, locates subsurface irrigation, and determines fractions of layers below the external and tile water tables. BeginMassCheck records starting storage. Surface preparation and hydraulic initialization follow.

M = 1 … NPH
Δt = dts_HeatWatTP = 0.99999 / NPH
dts_wat = min(1, 10 Δt) × 0.99999

Δt is a fraction of the hourly interval. dts_wat is a separate factor used in availability limits and relaxation terms; it should not be treated as interchangeable with Δt. The loop runs a fixed number of substeps, without a convergence test.

  1. Reset this substep

    PrepHydroThermIterM clears temporary flux arrays and saves surface-layer information.

  2. Solve the surface exchanges

    RunSurfacePhysModelM supplies infiltration and heat at the top of the soil, and updates the water available for internal flow.

  3. Calculate internal and boundary transfers

    Subsurface3DInternalFlowM visits internal faces; XBoundaryFlowM handles external faces.

  4. Assemble layer budgets and phase changes

    Summarize3DFlowM sums incoming minus outgoing face transfers, calculates pore-domain exchange, then calls FreezeThawIterateM. Optional snow-redistribution accumulation follows.

  5. Update water, ice, temperature, and surface states

    UpdateSoilMoistTempM applies transfers, irrigation, phase change, and the previously supplied root flux. Runoff aggregation and surface-state updates follow.

  6. Save intermediate states and check conservation

    Nonfinal steps call UpdateStateFluxAtM; the final step calls UpdateFluxAtExit. Every step ends with ExitMassBalanceM.

Source: WatsubMod.F90:82–206; time-step definitions in StartsMod.F90, set_ecosim_solver.

03 / Water reaches the soil through the surface model

WatsubMod delegates the detailed surface calculation to SurfPhysMod and its snow/litter collaborators. Surface preparation partitions precipitation and surface cover, establishes resistances, and prepares radiation and snow states.

Water

Partition and route

Surface substeps solve snow and exposed-surface exchanges, move water between litter and soil, partition infiltration and runoff, and optionally redistribute snow. Internal surface runoff is skipped in ATS coupling mode.

Energy

Pass heat with water

Precipitation, vapor exchange, and snow/litter transfers carry energy. The surface model also returns ground heat and a temperature derivative for the top-layer energy update.

The resulting top-face transfers are Qinfl2MicP, Qinfl2MacP, and HeatInfl2Soil. The micropore surface transfer includes signed vapor-related terms, so it should not always be interpreted as rainfall infiltration alone. RainEkReducedKsat scales surface hydraulic conductivity to account for rainfall kinetic-energy effects.

Source: StageSurfacePhysModel and RunSurfacePhysModelM in SurfPhysMod.F90.

04 / Internal flow connects neighboring layers and cells

The code sweeps west to east, north to south, and top to bottom. It processes the positive-direction face of each cell, skips domain boundaries, and checks whether source and destination layers are active. FlowDirIndicator_col controls which direction loops are visited.

Micropore liquid flow: potential differences and conductivity

Ψtotal = Ψmatric + Ψosmotic + Ψgravity
Ψgravity = mGravAccelerat × (elevation − layer midpoint depth)
G = 2 Kₛ K𝒹 / (Kₛ ℓ𝒹 + K𝒹 ℓₛ)
Qpotential = G × (Ψtotal,ₛ − Ψtotal,𝒹) × A × Δt

Here s and d denote source and destination, ℓ is the cell dimension along the flow direction, and A is face area. G combines the two hydraulic conductivities and path lengths. Conductivity is selected from the moisture-indexed HydroCond_3D lookup table prepared upstream.

MicropXGridDarcyFlow distinguishes four cases using matric potential relative to air-entry potential: both cells saturated, source only saturated, destination only saturated, or neither saturated. If one cell is saturated, the routine uses the auxiliary wetting-front state and air-entry water content in a Green–Ampt-style adjustment. Otherwise the gradient calculation uses the current moisture state.

The potential transfer is limited by donor liquid water and receiver air space, with dts_wat factors. A separate correction permits upward release from an overfilled lower layer. The donor-availability array VLWatMicP2_vr is updated as faces are processed, so subsequent face calculations see earlier liquid transfers. This is a sequential calculation.

Macropore liquid flow: a separate pathway

The implemented formula uses gravity plus a pore-water-level contribution, macropore conductance, donor water, and receiver space. Vertical flow additionally considers incoming macropore flow and overfilling. In the current snapshot this internal-flow branch is unreachable; its returned water and heat transfers are zero. Do not interpret the formula’s presence as evidence that this pathway is active.

Water vapor: temperature and water potential both matter

v = vapsat(T) × exp[18 (Ψmatric + Ψosmotic) / (RGASC × T)]
Qv,potential = Gv × (vₛ − v𝒹) × A × Δt

WaterVaporXgridFlow requires sufficient air-filled pore fraction in both cells. Effective vapor conductance depends on diffusivity, air-filled fraction, porosity, tortuosity, and distance. The transfer is bounded by an equilibrium-vapor calculation and available liquid water. Vapor transfer is added to the micropore water flux and carries sensible plus latent heat.

Exchange within a layer

Summarize3DFlowM calculates FWatExMacP2MicPiM_vr using the difference between saturation potential and current micropore matric potential, saturated horizontal conductivity, pore radius, and pore spacing. Positive exchange adds water to micropores and removes the same amount from macropores; negative exchange reverses the direction. It is bounded using provisional post-flow storage and available pore space. The routine enters this calculation only when existing macropore water exceeds its threshold.

Source: Subsurface3DInternalFlowM, MicropXGridDarcyFlow, MacropXgridFLow, WaterVaporXgridFlow, and Summarize3DFlowM.

A face orientation is not a flow direction

At west/north faces, XN=+1: positive stored face flux enters the cell. At east/south/bottom faces, XN=−1: positive stored face flux leaves. In either case, the boundary’s contribution to cell storage is XN × Qface. A negative flux reverses the direction.

Explore the sign convention

This is bookkeeping only, not a hydrology solver. The value is transferred volume per substep.

For free drainage, HydGrad = −XN × gravity × |slope| × area. Thus the formula attempts outward drainage on either side: a negative face flux at west/north and a positive one at east/south. The current west/north availability limiter has a sign problem; see the implementation notes.

Source: XBoundaryFlowM, Config4WaterTableDrain, Config4TileDrainage, DischargeOverWaterTBL, and RechargeFromExtWaterTBL.

06 / Heat changes the liquid–ice partition

Liquid transport carries sensible heat using the donor temperature. Vapor transport also carries latent heat. SolveXgridHeatConduction computes a conductive exchange from effective thermal conductance and the temperature difference, limited using a two-cell equilibrium-temperature estimate. Surface forcing and the deep boundary add further energy exchanges.

FreezeThawIterateM first forms provisional liquid volumes after water flow, pore exchange, and irrigation. It computes an apparent temperature from old energy plus incoming heat and the provisional heat capacity. Micropore freezing temperature comes from get_Tfrez using matric plus osmotic potential. Macropores use TFICE, without that depression.

The macropore calculation precedes the micropore calculation, but both use the same apparent temperature; the code does not recompute that temperature between the two. Each phase-change amount is bounded by available water or ice and uses a dts_wat relaxation factor.

ΔWthaw = −Hfreeze / LtHeatIceMelt
Wnew = Wprovisional + ΔWthaw
Inew = Iold − ΔWthaw / DENSICE

Positive ΔWthaw means melting: liquid increases, ice decreases, and latent heat is absorbed. Negative values mean freezing. Density conversion keeps liquid-equivalent water conserved even though liquid and ice occupy different volumes. The temperature update includes the corresponding phase-change heat.

Source: SolveXgridHeatConduction, FreezeThawIterateM, and UpdateSoilMoistTempM.

07 / Apply the layer budget, then carry it forward

The main water update can be summarized as follows. These are volume increments over a substep; the root term is applied later in the routine.

Wμ,new = Wμ,old + Qnet,μ + Qmacro→micro + ΔWthaw,μ + Qirrigation + Qroot,applied
WM,new = WM,old + Qnet,M − Qmacro→micro + ΔWthaw,M
Qnet = Σdirections (Qincoming face − Qoutgoing face)

Qnet,μ includes the assembled liquid and vapor transfers. Subsurface irrigation enters micropores in the layer containing the prescribed irrigation depth, with heat based on air temperature. Root water exchange is supplied through TWaterPlantRoot2SoilPrev_vr and distributed across substeps; root uptake physiology itself is outside this module.

Negative root flux removes water, positive flux returns water. If a withdrawal would cross below zero from positive micropore storage, the code scales that withdrawal and its associated updates. This conditional limiter is not a general nonnegativity guarantee for all hydrologic transfers.

Heat capacity is reconstructed from solids, liquid, and ice, with an optional living-root contribution. Temperature follows the energy budget; the surface layer includes the returned surface-flux temperature derivative. Root water additions and removals have separate heat accounting.

Between substeps, EcoSIM records moisture, air space, and flux histories used by transport and biogeochemistry. If a surface pond/lake layer has too little heat capacity, UpdateStateFluxAtM can transfer its remaining water, ice, and energy to a deeper meaningful layer and move the active top index. After the loop, working water, ice, and temperature are copied back to persistent arrays.

Special mode: fixed water level

fixWaterLevel sets HeatAdv_scal to zero, causes early returns in internal liquid/vapor flow routines, and skips selected flow accumulation and net-water updates. It is a special lake/water-level treatment, not the ordinary freely evolving soil-water calculation. Energy and phase-change code still run, and the cumulative mass-check expression differs.

Source: LocalCopySoilVars, InitSoilHydrauics, UpdateSoilMoistTempM, UpdateStateFluxAtM, and the end of watsub.

08 / Conservation is checked at every substep

The checked soil/water-column storage is liquid plus liquid-equivalent ice over active layers:

S = Σlayers [Wμ + WM + DENSICE × (Iμ + IM)]

For ordinary mode, the cumulative residual is:

R = Sstart − Scurrent
  + Qsurface + Qlateral + Qroot
  − Qwater-table − Qdrainage

These are signed diagnostics: for example, a negative water-table “discharge” represents recharge. Snow and litter storage are outside this particular column sum; their exchanges enter through the surface transfer. Internal vertical transfers and within-layer pore exchange should cancel in the column total; freezing and thawing should cancel after density conversion.

ExitMassBalanceM evaluates both a substep and a cumulative residual and aborts when the absolute residual exceeds 1.e-4 in the code’s liquid-equivalent volume accounting. It does not divide this threshold by cell area. Subsurface irrigation is added directly in the state update but is not an explicit standalone term in these displayed residual expressions; the formula here documents the check as written.

A closed budget is necessary, but not sufficient. A transfer can be incorrectly large yet balance algebraically if the same amount is removed from storage and recorded as drainage. Passing this check does not demonstrate positive storage, correct flow direction, or scientific accuracy.

Source: BeginMassCheck and ExitMassBalanceM, WatsubMod.F90:210–289,356–375.

09 / Qualifications for this code snapshot

The process explanation above follows the current implementation. The following observations come from source review and isolated arithmetic checks, not a full model validation.

Internal macropore transport is disabled

MacropXgridFLow assigns LInvalidMacP=.true. immediately before requiring .not.LInvalidMacP. Its internal transport branch cannot execute, even outside fixed-water-level mode. Whether this disabling is desired is a model-policy question; the zero internal transfer is definite code behavior. Location: WatsubMod.F90:2032–2034.

West/north free drainage has an ineffective availability limit

The expression MIN(positive available water, negative hydraulic flow) always selects the negative flow. It can therefore drain an empty macropore when conductivity and boundary scaling are positive. This concerns enabled free lateral drainage, not every water-table exchange. Negative XN does not have this particular sign defect. Location: WatsubMod.F90:1232–1237.

Micropore tile discharge uses the external-table depth

The shared discharge routine accepts TargetWaterTBL, but its micropore pressure expression uses ExtWaterTable_col. Tile eligibility and the tile layer fraction can therefore be combined with a natural-table pressure gradient. The macropore expression uses the supplied target. Location: WatsubMod.F90:2303–2305.

Cold-start ice can depend on how liquid water was specified

Ice initialization subtracts raw THW from porosity rather than resolved THETW. Since THW=1 means field capacity, equivalent liquid inputs can produce different initial ice. This occurs in SoilHydroParaMod.F90:365–369 and in the supplied-parameter path in StartsMod.F90.

Intentional initialization policy. If either field capacity or wilting point is missing, EcoSIM estimates both as a consistent pair. Estimated FC is capped at 0.75 × porosity, then estimated WP at 0.75 × FC. Both supplied values are retained when neither is missing. This paired reset is intentional and is not a review finding.
