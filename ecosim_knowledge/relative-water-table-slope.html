How closely does the water table follow the terrain?

EcoSIM’s relative slope r is the ratio of water-table elevation gradient to ground elevation gradient. The diagram anchors both surfaces at the left-hand reference location, then shows how their separation changes uphill.

Elevation and depth use different factors

h(x) = href + Sx
Hw(x) = href − D + rSx
dw(x) = D + (1 − r)Sx

Here S = tan(θ), the signed horizontal ground gradient along this uphill transect. Because dw = h − Hw, r controls water-table elevation change, while 1 − r controls depth change.

At r = 0, the water table is horizontal. At r = 1, it is parallel to the terrain. Values above 1 illustrate a water table rising faster than the terrain.

Connection to EcoSIM. The baseline depth conversion is D − (h_ref − h)(1 − r). It appears in StartsMod.F90, in InitSoilVars, currently as NaturalWaterTableDepzTopoC_col (previously ExtWaterTablet0_col). The input parameter is WaterTable2GroundSlope_col, read from DTBLGG.

This illustrates prescribed geometry with constant r and reference depth D. It does not simulate water movement, pressure profiles, soil-surface datum offsets, or mobile water-table updates. When ground is flat, both surfaces remain horizontal regardless of r; their slope ratio is then undefined, although the parameter can still be supplied.
