How nitrifiers connect
ammonia, nitrite, and N₂O

EcoSIM represents ammonia oxidizers and nitrite oxidizers as separate autotrophic functional groups. Both obtain energy from nitrogen transformations and take biomass carbon from CO₂. Ammonia oxidizers also have a nitrite-reduction pathway when oxygen supply falls short of demand.

Implementation snapshot · 29 September 2026 · solphys, base commit 03f68ad5 plus the working-tree NH₄ donor-limit and denitrification-maintenance fixes. Primary source: NitrifierMod.F90. This is a guide to the implemented equations, not a general nitrification literature review.

1. Two functional groups, three transformations

AUTOTROPHIC GROUP 1

Ammonia oxidation

NH₃ + 1.5 O₂ → NO₂⁻ + H⁺ + H₂O

AmmoniaOxidizerCatabolism calculates potential oxidation. The shared oxygen solver determines its realized fraction.

AUTOTROPHIC GROUP 2

Nitrite oxidation

NO₂⁻ + 0.5 O₂ → NO₃⁻

NitriteOxidizerCatabolism calculates a separate rate, biomass response, and NO₂ allocation.

ADDITIONAL GROUP 1 PATHWAY

Nitrifier denitrification

2 NO₂⁻ + NH₃ → 1.5 N₂O + 2 OH⁻ + 0.5 H₂O

AmmoniaOxiDenitCatabolism couples additional ammonia consumption to nitrite reduction.

The last reaction is the model’s coded stoichiometry. Its N₂O contains nitrogen from both ammonia and nitrite. It is distinct from heterotrophic denitrification in DenitrifierMod, and from the nitrite-dependent methane oxidation in MethanotrophMod. This nitrifier module does not implement a further N₂O-to-N₂ reaction.

Sources: MicBGCPars: group IDs and activation; three catabolic routines.

2. State, compartments, and execution order

Each functional group can contain multiple guilds, indexed by NGL from JGniA(N) to JGnfA(N). mBiomeAutor stores microbial C, N, and P in kinetic, structural, and reserve compartments; OMActAutor is the active-C quantity driving metabolic capacity. Catabolism skips guilds with nonpositive active biomass.

Mineral donors are split into fertilizer-band and nonband pools. ZNH4S/B and ZNO2S/B are N pools; CNH3S/B, CNH4S/B, and CNO2S/B are concentrations. The ammonia oxidation kinetics use NH₃ concentration, but their donor cap uses the NH₄ pool supplied to this microbial interface. These variables cannot be substituted for one another. NH₄ fractions VLNH4/VLNHB and NO₂ fractions VLNO3/VLNOB can differ.

PrepareStage biomass, temperature/water response, nutrient status, maintenance, and lagged competition.
OxidizeCalculate NH₃ or NO₂ potential oxidation; apply the common O₂ uptake solver.
Reduce & allocateFor ammonia oxidizers, calculate nitrite reduction. Then calculate mineral nutrition, maintenance, and anabolic fluxes.
Update & exportUpdate biomass and residue pools; aggregate nitrogen and gas fluxes; pass them through the API.

ActiveAutotrophs performs the dispatch and shared calculations. The layer driver later calls AutotrophAnabolicUpdate and AggregateTransfOMBioResdue. Nitrifier denitrification therefore sees realized, oxygen-limited aerobic ammonia oxidation from the current call. Its fixed RTotNH3OxidSoilAutor/BandAutor totals represent NO₂ production by aerobic oxidation only.

Sources: ActiveAutotrophs; SoilBGCOneLayer; pool and concentration mapping.

3. Potential oxidation: biomass, environment, and donors

For compact notation below, B means active microbial C, E is GrowthEnvScalAutor, S is FBiomNutStoiScalAutor, and W is VOLWZ. Subscript j denotes one band or nonband compartment. Fluxes are amounts per model hourly step on the layer’s spatial basis. Nitrogen transformations use N mass, carbon terms use C mass, and oxygen demand uses O₂ mass.

fCO2 = CCO2S / (CCO2S + CCKM)

Ammonia oxidizers:
  Vraw = VMXNH3Oxi × E × S × fCO2 × B
  V = Vraw / [1 + Vraw/(VHKI × W)]       (V = 0 when W is too small)
  Kj = V × NH4_compartment_fractionj × CNH3j/(CNH3j + ZHKM)
  Pj = max(0, min[Kj, FNH4j × ZNH4j]) × inhibition_multiplierj

Nitrite oxidizers:
  V = VMXNO2Oxi × E × S × fCO2 × B
  Kj = V × NO2_compartment_fractionj × CNO2j/(CNO2j + ZNKM)
  Pj = max(0, min[Kj, FNO2j × ZNO2j])

The NH₃ rate attenuation involving VHKI is labeled product inhibition in the source. It depends on potential rate per water volume; the expression does not directly use product concentration. NH₃ oxidation also has a fertilizer-related multiplier based on ZNFN0, evolving ZNFNI, temperature, and CNH4/ZHKI; without an active inhibition reference the multiplier is 1.

Competition is based on previous demand

F = max(FMN, guild demand from previous hour / ecosystem demand from previous hour)
If previous ecosystem demand is negligible:
  NH4: F = max(FMN, FracOMActAutor × NH4 compartment fraction)
  NO2: F = max(FMN, FracNO2XupAutor × NO2 compartment fraction)

These are lagged allocations, not a simultaneous solve over all organisms. FMN supplies a minimum share; that floor alone does not normalize shares. RNH3OxidAutor and RNO2XupAutor are competition-demand arrays, while RSMetaOxidSoilAutor/BandAutor hold realized oxidation after O₂ limitation. The oxygen solver also scales the aerobic competition demands by its limiter. The denitrification routine then adds its potential NH₄ demand to the ammonia-oxidizer demand arrays.

Sources: AmmoniaOxidizerCatabolism, NitriteOxidizerCatabolism; StageAutotroph.

4. What the oxygen limiter means

For total potential oxidation P = Psoil + Pband, the code calculates a gross-respiration C equivalent and the combined oxygen demand:

η = EO2X
Ammonia oxidizers: Rpotential = P × ECNH × η
                   O2 demand = 3.429 × P + 2.667 × Rpotential
Nitrite oxidizers:  Rpotential = P × ECNO × η
                   O2 demand = 1.143 × P + 2.667 × Rpotential

fLimO2Autor = min(1, max(0, realized O2 uptake / potential O2 demand))
Realized oxidation = potential oxidation × fLimO2Autor
RespGrossAutor = Rpotential × fLimO2Autor

1 means no oxygen restriction; 0 means no aerobic activity supported by O₂. This is an actual-to-potential ratio, not the fraction inhibited. The coefficients 3.429 and 1.143 convert N oxidation to O₂ demand; 2.667 converts respiratory C to O₂ demand. The NH₃ routine contains a nearby comment with 3.249, but the executable equation uses 3.429.

AerobicAutorO2Uptake uses competition-allocated aqueous and gas O₂, diffusion through water films, and gas–water exchange over NPH × NPT substeps. It clips each uptake to dissolved O₂ available at that substep; dissolution afterward supplies the next substep. The kinetic response uses OXKA. The surface-litter branch with negligible micropore volume bypasses this transport solve and assigns a limiter of 1. Nitrifier denitrification is gated off under that same litter condition.

Source: AerobicAutorO2Uptake and ActiveAutotrophs.

5. Nitrifier denitrification and the two-donor cap

The additional pathway is driven by the unmet respiration-associated O₂ demand, rather than the whole oxidation-plus-respiration oxygen demand. It requires active biomass and positive potential respiration demand.

O2 deficit = max(0, RO2Dmnd4GrossRespAutor − RO2Uptk4RespAutor)
Vden = 0.875 × O2 deficit × fCO2
Kj = NO2_compartment_fractionj × Vden × CNO2j/(CNO2j + Z2KM)
Kj ← Kj / [1 + (Ksoil + Kband)/(VMKI × W)]
       (both rates are zero when W is too small)

NO2availablej = FNO2j × max(0, ZNO2j + total aerobic NH3 oxidationj)

The current-hour aerobic NO₂ production is snapshotted before looping over guilds. Additional NH₄ sent to N₂O is never counted as new NO₂ production.

Reserve remaining NH₄ separately in each compartment

At loop entry:
  NH4remainingj = max(0, ZNH4j − sum of aerobic NH3 oxidationj over guilds)
For each guild:
  NH4availablej = max(0, min[NH4remainingj,
                           FNH4j × ZNH4j − this guild's aerobic NH3 oxidationj])
  Dj = max(0, min[Kj, NO2availablej, 2 × NH4availablej])
  NH4remainingj ← max(0, NH4remainingj − 0.5 × Dj)

Additional NH4-N consumed = 0.5 × Dj
NO2-N consumed = Dj
N2O-N produced = 1.5 × Dj

The NH₄ share is reconstructed using the same lagged allocation as aerobic ammonia oxidation. Consequently, the two pathways share one guild allocation. The running compartment remainder also prevents subsequent guilds from reusing NH₄ already consumed by denitrification if allocations overlap. It does not replace the model’s broader competition scheme or retroactively correct earlier uptake by other processes.

The recent fix adds the 2 × NH4available bound before deriving respiration and growth supply. A nonband compartment with zero NH₄ now supports zero nitrifier denitrification there, even if fertilizer-band NH₄ supports layer-wide potential respiration. Potential NH₄ demand for the next hour includes 0.5 × Kj; potential NO₂ demand remains Kj.

Source: AmmoniaOxiDenitCatabolism.

6. Carbon assimilation, maintenance, and nutrients

The catabolic routines supply the shared autotrophic growth machinery. Nitrogen oxidation provides the energy proxy; carbon comes from environmental CO₂. Mineral NH₄/NO₃ and phosphate exchanges needed for biomass stoichiometry are calculated separately from redox substrate consumption. Thus, ammonia oxidizers can consume NH₄ both as an energy substrate and as a biomass nutrient.

R = realized aerobic gross respiration; M = maintenance demand
Rd = (Dsoil + Dband) × ECNO × ENOX     [ammonia oxidizers only]
Primary growth respiration = max(0, R − M)
α = (1/ENOX − 1) / (1/EO2X − 1)     [default ≈ 0.2667]
Md = min(Rd, max(0,M−R)/α)           [denitrification respiration reserved for maintenance]
Maintenance deficit = max(0, M − R − α × Md)

C uptake U = min(M,R) + max(0,R−M)/EO2X + Md + (Rd−Md)/ENOX
C added to reserves G = U − R − Rd
                     = max(0,R−M) × (1/EO2X − 1) + (Rd−Md) × (1/ENOX − 1)

These simplified equations follow the corrected shared routines; autotrophic N₂ fixation is set to zero there. For the zero-energy limit ENOX = 1, denitrification supplies no maintenance energy and Md is zero; the helper avoids division by α = 0. DOMuptk4GrothAutor(ielmc,NGL) stores U, despite its DOM-oriented name. For nitrifiers, RCO2XumpAutor = U. Aerobic CO₂ production and the denitrification respiratory term are aggregated separately, so metabolic net CO₂ uptake is U − R − Rd = G before mortality and other transfers.

Reserve C/N/P can be allocated to kinetic and structural biomass. Ordinary mortality and maintenance-starvation losses generate recycling, microbial residues, and humified material. Those transfers are required for a complete biomass budget.

Maintenance before growth. ReserveDenitrifMaintenance first credits primary respiration, then reserves denitrification energy for the remaining maintenance demand. The reference conversion α follows the existing EO2X and ENOX yield relations, which share the growth-energy cost EOMC; the default ratio is 10/37.5. It preserves the primary pathway’s existing maintenance convention. Only unreserved denitrification respiration earns growth-associated C uptake, and only the remaining deficit triggers starvation. Previously, all denitrification respiration supported growth while maintenance ignored it. The same correction covers heterotrophic denitrifiers, with the reservation distributed proportionally between DOC and acetate respiration.

Sources: GatherAutotrophRespiration, GatherAutotrophAnabolicFlux, AutotrophAnabolicUpdate; AggregateTransfOMBioResdue.

7. Follow the nitrogen to the API

For one compartment, let A be realized aerobic ammonia-N oxidation, B aerobic nitrite-N oxidation, and D nitrite-N reduction by nitrifiers. Isolating these reactions from assimilation and other organisms gives:

PoolChange from nitrifier redox reactionsMeaning
NH₄-N−A − 0.5DAmmonia used by aerobic oxidation and nitrifier denitrification.
NO₂-N+A − B − DAerobic ammonia product, less oxidation and reduction.
NO₃-N+BProduct of aerobic nitrite oxidation.
N₂O-N+1.5DNitrite N plus additional ammonia N.
Total N0All four changes sum to zero.

After denitrification, the ammonia group’s RSMetaOxidSoilAutor/BandAutor contains A + 0.5D. Aggregation subtracts 0.5D when crediting aerobic NO₂ production, preventing the additional ammonia-to-N₂O transfer from becoming a false nitrite source. TNitNO2Redux2N2OSoil/Band records 1.5D, the full N₂O-N production.

RNut_MicbRelease_vr exports net mineral exchange with positive values meaning release to soil. trcs_RMicbUptake_vr exports gas exchange with positive values meaning uptake, so N₂O production contributes a negative gas-uptake flux. Whole-layer exports include other microbial pathways and nutrient assimilation; they need not equal the isolated reaction terms in this table.

Sources: AggregateTransfOMBioResdue; gas/mineral export; next-hour ecosystem demand accumulation.

8. Explore the NH₄ constraint

One guild, one compartment

This algebraic example starts with an NH₄ allocation H, of which fraction a was already consumed aerobically. It assumes the shared compartment remainder is no tighter than this guild’s allocation. Q is allocated NO₂ including any credited aerobic production; K is kinetic NO₂-reduction demand. Values use common N-mass units per hourly step. This is not a full EcoSIM simulation.

D = min(K, Q, 2H(1−a))
Extra NH4 consumption = D/2; N2O production = 1.5D
Realized NO₂-N reduction D0.200
Additional NH₄-N consumed0.100
N₂O-N produced0.300

Remaining NH₄ limits the reaction. NH₄ remaining after the reaction: 0.000.

Without the NH₄ cap, D would be 0.600 and require 0.300 NH₄-N; only 0.100 remains allocated.

The NH₄ correction was checked with 1,134 source-extracted Fortran scenarios covering donor exhaustion, separate compartments, overlapping allocations, inactive guilds, respiration coupling, and N₂O-N balance. The pre-fix routine failed the zero-NH₄ regression case. The build passed; 16 example namelists completed 20 days (480 steps each), excluding dryland2, and 19 history files passed finite-output checks. These results validate the tested donor accounting and short-run execution, not the full model’s scientific accuracy or long-term behavior.
