Cyanobacteria:
light, maintenance, and residual growth

EcoSIM represents cyanobacteria as mixotrophic microbes: they fix CO₂ using light, consume dissolved organic carbon and acetate, and can fix N₂. Photosynthetic fixation supplies maintenance first. Carbon left after respiratory costs is available for growth.

Carbon/O₂ guide originally prepared 29 September 2026 · PAR documentation updated 30 September 2026 against f0314ff4 · solphys. The light section reflects the incident-PAR and sequential shading implementation in MicBGCAPI.F90.

1. Three coupled resources

CARBON

Two entry routes

CO₂ fixation supplies photosynthate. DOC and acetate supply organic C. The organic donor pools are debited separately from photosynthetic input.

OXYGEN

Production and consumption

Photosynthetic O₂ supports respiration internally. Only the remaining demand competes for soil O₂; unused photosynthetic O₂ is exported.

NUTRITION

Growth needs N and P

N₂ fixation can supplement nitrogen uptake at a carbon cost. Phosphorus must still come from organic or mineral nutrient pools.

The central allocation rule: photosynthetic fixation is the maximum photosynthetic C supply, not a prescribed respiration flux. The code uses min(maintenance demand, fixation) for photosynthate-supported maintenance. It does not apply the organic-C respiration fraction ENFX to the remaining photosynthate.

Sources: CyanoBacteriaCatabolism; shared respiration and anabolic routines.

2. Where cyanobacteria live in the model

The functional-group identifier is mid_HeterMixtCynoBacter = 8, with one guild by default. Cyanobacteria use the heterotrophic state and dispatch machinery despite their photosynthetic capability. The loop over NGL supports multiple guilds within each organic-matter complex K.

mBiomeHeter(element, MID, K) stores C, N, and P in three living compartments: kinetic, structural, and reserve. MID combines compartment and guild indices. Metabolic capacity uses OMActHeter, derived from kinetic C divided by its allocation fraction, rather than simply summing all three C pools. Nonpositive active biomass skips catabolism.

PrepareStage active biomass, nutrient status, environment, competition, and maintenance demand.
MetabolizeCalculate photosynthesis and potential organic respiration; apply O₂ supply.
AllocateReserve maintenance, calculate N₂ fixation and donor-specific organic uptake.
UpdateUpdate reserves, structural/kinetic biomass and residues; export gas and nutrient fluxes.

Initialization

InoculateCyanoBacter initializes cyanobacteria in litter (L = 0) and the first active soil layer (L = NU) for complexes with positive organic substrate. It distributes CyanoInocC using the compartment/complex allocation OMCI(M,K)/KL, divides among guilds, and assigns N and P from target ratios. By default it replaces those cyanobacterial pools; an optional argument permits addition.

CyanoInocC defaults to 0.01 g C m⁻² as declared in the parameter module. The separate CyanoInoc_depth = 0.001 m parameter is currently not consumed by the inoculation routine: it does not establish an explicit 1 mm living layer.

Sources: group and guild definitions; inoculation; active biomass and StageFuncGuild.

3. Light, CO₂, water, and temperature set fixation

Let I be incident PAR at the layer’s upper interface in μmol photons m⁻² s⁻¹; B the nonnegative product of active C and the N/P stoichiometric multiplier; and E the growth environment multiplier. Carbon fluxes below share the model layer's mass-per-hour basis; they should not be read as per-individual rates or automatically as per-m² output.

fPAR = max(0, α I (1 − β I) / (1 + γ I))
fCO₂ = CCO2S / (CCO2S + CCKM)
rphoto = fPAR × fCO₂ × E                       [h⁻¹]
P = RCO2FixCyano = rphoto × B                  [C mass h⁻¹]

α = 1.1 × 10⁻⁴ h⁻¹ per unit PAR
β = 3.3 × 10⁻⁴ per unit PAR; γ = 1.5 × 10⁻³ per unit PAR
E = WSensGroHeter × TSensGroHeter
WSensGroHeter = exp[0.2 × max(PSISoilMatricP, −500)]

In darkness, fixation is zero. The empirical light function rises and then declines; its nonnegative clipping gives zero at and above roughly 3030 μmol photons m⁻² s⁻¹. This describes the coded parameterization. The CO₂ term is a concentration saturation response, not a separate cap on the available CO₂ mass within this routine. Temperature uses the common microbial growth response. Nutrient status multiplies capacity through min(FCN,FCP), whose component multipliers are bounded between 0.5 and 1.

How PAR is passed through the column

MicrobeModel processes the column from the surface downward. WatsubMod has already weighted the litter-covered and bare-soil PAR streams by their surface-cover fractions. Their sum, divided by cell area, supplies PAR_RAD_incident to MicBGC1Layer; the microbial caller does not apply those cover fractions again.

A = cell area
Iincident = (Slitter + Sbare) / A

1. Run the current layer’s microbial biology using Iincident.
2. Sum cyanobacterial C after that biological update: Bcyano.
3. τ = k × layer thickness + k_cyanoC × Bcyano / A
4. T = sfexp(−τ)                       [exponential transmission]

Litter: Slitter,out = Slitter × T
        Sbare,out  = Sbare             [bare-soil stream bypasses litter]
Soil:   Slitter,out = Slitter × T
        Sbare,out  = Sbare × T

Inext = (Slitter,out + Sbare,out) / A

The streams Slitter and Sbare above denote the cell-total PAR contributions; division by A produces irradiance per unit area. Litter uses k_litr = 250 m⁻¹. Soil uses CalcKSoilPAR, which depends on organic C and texture and is bounded between 800 and 10,000 m⁻¹. Cyanobacterial self-shading uses k_cyanoC = 0.1 m² g⁻¹ C. The code evaluates transmission with the protected exponential sfexp.

The current layer receives incoming light before its own attenuation. Its updated cyanobacterial biomass then determines shading of deeper layers. This is the intended sequential surface-to-depth coupling. The nonnegative PAR_RAD_vr diagnostic records incident PAR for the active layer. Microbial biology still runs at night with zero PAR.

Incident, mean, and absorbed light are different quantities

For a single attenuating stream with upper-interface irradiance I₀, bottom transmission is I₀ exp(−τ); the irradiance removed from that stream is I₀[1−exp(−τ)]. A layer mean under uniform exponential attenuation would instead be I₀[1−exp(−τ)]/τ (approaching I₀ as τ approaches zero). The current microbial calculation uses incident irradiance, not either of these latter quantities.

This represents cyanobacterial exposure at the layer’s upper interface. If cyanobacteria were instead represented as distributed throughout a thick layer, their exposure and nonlinear photosynthetic response would need a corresponding depth-distribution assumption. That is a modeling choice to assess for the application, rather than an error in the adopted surface-to-depth update.

Sources: fPAR_func and catabolism; MicrobeModel incident PAR and sequential shading; CalcKSoilPAR; sfexp.

4. Maintenance first; growth is the residual

Maintenance demand M is calculated before catabolism from kinetic N and active structural N, with compartment factors, temperature, and a pH multiplier. Photosynthetic maintenance respiration is Rph = min(M,P). If fixation cannot meet maintenance, organic respiration supplies the remaining requirement.

pH multiplier = 1 + max(0, 0.25 × (6.5 − pH))
M = RMOM × temperature response × pH multiplier
    × [kinetic N × RMOMK(1) + active structural N × RMOMK(2)]

Rph = min(M, P)
Remaining maintenance demand = max(0, M − P)

Potential organic respiration

DOC and acetate metabolism is downregulated as the photosynthetic rate rises. Its additional day/night factor is 0.1 with positive PAR and 0.01 in darkness. The classification depends on PAR, even if the high-light clamp makes fixation zero.

f_low_photo = 0.006 / (rphoto + 0.006)          (1 in darkness)
V = B × VMXO × E × f_low_photo × day/night factor

Hd,pot = min(DOC pool × FOQC × ENFX,
             V × CDOC/(CDOC + OQKM) × FOCA)
Ha,pot = min(acetate pool × FOQA × EO2A,
             V × CAcet/(CAcet + OQKA) × FOAA)
Hpot = Hd,pot + Ha,pot
wd = Hd,pot/Hpot; wa = Ha,pot/Hpot             (when Hpot > 0)
H = Hpot × oxygen limiter

FOCA/FOAA describe the DOC/acetate composition of the dissolved substrate. FOQC/FOQA allocate donor pools using previous-hour guild/ecosystem demand, with a minimum FMN share; active-biomass fractions provide the fallback. These lagged, floored shares are not a simultaneous normalized allocation. wd/wa, stored as FGOCP/FGOAP, partition organic respiration only.

Convert respiration to actual donor withdrawal

For the simplest case without N₂ fixation, define organic maintenance Mh = min(H,max(0,M−P)) and organic growth respiration Hg = H−Mh. Each donor retains its own respiration fraction:

Ud = wd × (Mh + Hg/ENFX)                      DOC-C withdrawal
Ua = wa × (Mh + Hg/EO2A)                      acetate-C withdrawal
R  = Rph + H                                 gross primary respiration
Utotal = P + Ud + Ua                          total C input
G = Utotal − R                               net C gain before turnover
CO₂ release = R − P                          negative = net uptake

Carbon balance: G + CO₂ release − Ud − Ua = 0

Maintenance C is respired without earning growth-associated uptake. Organic growth respiration is converted to uptake through ENFX or EO2A; the uptake left after respiration enters microbial reserves. All photosynthetic fixation is credited to C input, including when maintenance consumes it completely. DOMUptk4GrothHeter(C,...) therefore includes photosynthetic C despite its name.

Photosynthesis alone

P = 0.10, M = 0.04: respiration is 0.04 and growth supply is 0.06. Net CO₂ release is −0.06. No extra ENFX charge is applied to that 0.06.

Maintenance shortage

P = 0.03, M = 0.05, with no organic supply: photosynthate respiration is 0.03, new C gain is zero, and the 0.02 deficit enters the shared starvation calculation.

Sources: CalcRespMaintHeter; potential respiration; GatherHetertrophAnabolicFlux and biomass update.

5. Internal O₂ supply and signed external exchange

The code uses q = 2.667 mass units of O₂ per unit C for the photosynthesis/respiration coupling. Photosynthetic O₂ first covers photosynthate respiration; the surplus can support organic respiration. Only the remaining positive demand enters the common soil O₂ uptake solver.

S = q × max(0, P − Rph)                       internal O₂ surplus
Dext = max(0, q × Hpot − S)                   external demand
U = realized external uptake from the soil O₂ solver
L = min(1, (U + S)/(q × Hpot))                when Hpot > 0
H = Hpot × L

O₂ release = max(0, S − q × Hpot)
O₂net = U − O₂ release                       positive = external uptake
O₂net = q × (R − P)                          primary-metabolism identity

OxyLimterHeter measures realized versus potential organic respiration after including internal O₂: 1 means unrestricted, and 0 means unsupported. Organic respiration can therefore continue without external O₂ uptake when photosynthesis supplies enough oxygen. It is not the ratio of signed net O₂ exchange to total gross demand.

The generic uptake solver treats water-film diffusion, gas–liquid exchange, competition, and dissolved availability over transport substeps. Cyanobacterial O₂ production is handled outside that nonnegative uptake calculation. Unused production makes RO2UptkHeter negative and is also subtracted from REcoUptkSoilO2M, distributed across the hourly transport substeps. The transport interface retains that sign as gas production.

Gross oxygen demand and consumption remain separate diagnostics. The nonnegative external demand retained in RO2DmndHetert is used for subsequent competition; it must not be replaced by signed net exchange. The identity above covers the primary pathway and the explicit N₂-fixation extension below. Shared biomass starvation and turnover are separate accounting steps.

Sources: O₂ demand, limiter, and release; AerobicHeterO2Uptake; gas flux routing.

6. N₂ fixation spends available carbon and oxygen

Fixation responds to reserve N deficiency, dissolved N₂, and available metabolic capacity. For cyanobacteria, that capacity combines organic growth respiration with photosynthetic C left after maintenance. Consequently, RGrowthRespHeter in this branch includes unrespired photosynthate and is not itself a CO₂ emission.

Hg = max(0, R − M)                           before additional N₂-fixation respiration
Pg = max(0, P − Rph)
A = Hg + Pg
Q = max(0, reserve C × target N:C − reserve N) / EN2F(8)
Ftrial = min[A × Q/(A + Q) × CZ2GS/(CZ2GS + ZFKM),
             OMGR × reserve C]               when A is positive

Forg = min(Hg, Ftrial)
Fphoto = min(Pg, max(0, −O₂net)/q, max(0, Ftrial − Forg))
F = Forg + Fphoto
N₂-N fixation = F × EN2F(8)

Forg redirects organic growth respiration already counted in R. It does not respire that carbon a second time. Fphoto is additional photosynthate respiration and is limited by both residual C and unused photosynthetic O₂; this stage does not call a second external O₂ uptake solve.

The code adds Fphoto once to gross respiration and net CO₂ release, and adds q × Fphoto to net O₂ uptake and its substep budget. It updates fPhotoR accordingly. Donor withdrawals become Ud = wd × [Mh + Forg + (Hg−Forg)/ENFX] and the analogous acetate expression with EO2A. Thus organic C used for fixation earns no growth increment. Photosynthetic C gain is reduced by Fphoto. The later biomass update bypasses the generic extra fixation-respiration debit for cyanobacteria, avoiding double counting.

Source: GatherHeterotrophRespiration, GatherHetertrophAnabolicFlux, and heterotrophic biomass update.

7. Nutrients, reserve allocation, and losses

Organic N and P uptake accompanies organic C uptake, using dissolved substrate N:C and P:C ratios and the nutrient-status factors. Each is capped by its allocated DON or DOP pool. The shared mineral-nutrient routines provide NH₄, NO₃, and phosphate exchange, including band/nonband compartments. N₂ fixation supplements N; it supplies no phosphorus.

Net assimilated C enters the reserve compartment along with associated N and P. Transfer from reserves to kinetic and structural biomass depends on OMGR, the growth environment multiplier, allocation fractions, and nutrient stoichiometry. Positive C input therefore does not imply that all of it immediately becomes active biomass.

If realized respiration cannot cover maintenance, the remaining deficit drives the shared starvation pathway. Mortality and starvation redistribute C/N/P into recycled material and microbial residue pools; some recycled C is released as CO₂. These later terms must be included when comparing complete biomass and gas budgets. The cyanobacterial catabolic routine itself sets CH₄, acetate, and H₂ production to zero.

The model represents functional-group metabolism and shared microbial pools. This implementation does not resolve individual cells, filament geometry, heterocysts, or explicit oxygen protection of nitrogenase.

Source: mineral nutrition, reserve allocation, starvation, and residue aggregation.
