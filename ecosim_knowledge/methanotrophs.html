How methanotrophs consume methane

EcoSIM represents methane oxidation through three microbial functional groups. They share biomass, maintenance, and nutrient-allocation machinery, but use different electron acceptors and different routes for biomass carbon.

Code snapshot: working tree based on 318eda41, with the aerobic CH₄-budget correction · solphys · 29 September 2026. This document describes the inspected implementation, including the corrected aerobic carbon budget and the earlier mismatch for comparison.

1. Three pathways, three carbon routes

GROUP 3 · AEROBIC

O₂-dependent methane oxidizers

Oxidize CH₄ for energy and use additional CH₄ carbon for respiration and biomass synthesis.

AeroMethanotrophCatabolism

GROUP 6 · NC10

Nitrite-dependent oxidation

Couple CH₄ oxidation to NO₂⁻ reduction to N₂. The model takes biomass carbon from environmental CO₂.

AMONC10Catabolism

GROUP 4 · ANME-2d

Nitrate-dependent oxidation

Couple CH₄ oxidation to NO₃⁻ reduction to NO₂⁻. Some oxidation-derived CO₂ is reassimilated into biomass.

AMOANME2dCatabolism

All three use the code’s Autor arrays and “autotrophic” infrastructure. That implementation category does not mean that all three use external CO₂ as their biomass carbon source. Methanotrophs consume methane; the separate methanogens produce it.

Sources: MicBGCPars.F90: Init; MethanotrophMod.F90.

2. Biomass state and execution sequence

A functional group may contain multiple guilds, indexed by NGL between JGniA(N) and JGnfA(N). Each guild has C, N, and P in kinetic, structural, and reserve biomass compartments. mBiomeAutor stores these pools; OMActAutor supplies active biomass for metabolic rates. These arrays have no heterotrophic substrate-complex index K.

Prepare
Active biomass, temperature, water stress, competition, maintenance
→
Catabolism
CH₄ and acceptor availability determine potential rates
→
Growth
O₂ limitation if aerobic; nutrient exchange, maintenance and growth allocation
→
Update
Biomass, mortality, gas and mineral fluxes; API export to transport

ActiveAutotrophs dispatches to the appropriate methane-oxidation routine. Only the aerobic group subsequently calls AerobicAutorO2Uptake. Shared routines then calculate nutrient mobilization, respiration partitioning, and anabolic fluxes. The later biomass update and aggregation pass produces the net fluxes exported through MicBGCAPI.

Guilds with nonpositive active biomass are skipped in the catabolic routines. Group/guild configuration therefore matters as well as substrate availability.

Sources: ActiveAutotrophs, AutotrophAnabolicUpdate; biomass compartment identifiers. Related: microbial complexes.

3. Aerobic oxidation: methane first, oxygen second

The routine prepares a competition-weighted share of dissolved and gaseous CH₄. The share FCH4X uses the guild’s previous CH₄ demand divided by ecosystem demand, or its active-biomass fraction when previous demand is negligible, with a floor FMN. This lagged allocation is not a simultaneous competition solve.

The potential oxidation capacity combines active biomass, environmental response, nutrient status, and the specific oxidation rate:

V = OMActAutor × GrowthEnvScalAutor × FBiomNutStoiScalAutor × VMXCH4OxiAero
fCH4 = CCH4S1 / (CCH4S1 + CCK4)
η = EH4X

s = ECHO × η; b = 1/η − 1
Mremaining = max(0, hourly maintenance − respiration already accumulated)
H = 0.9999 × CH4S1
Pcap = H/(1+s)
If s × Pcap > Mremaining: Pcap = (H+b×Mremaining)/(1+s+b×s)
Vstep = V × dts_gas (soil); max(0, min[V × dts_gas, initial guild gas CH4]) (litter)
Pstep = max(0, min[Pcap, Vstep × fCH4])
Rstep = Pstep × s
Gstep = max(0, Rstep − Mremaining) × b
CH4S1 ← CH4S1 − Pstep − Rstep − Gstep

Here P is direct catabolic oxidation and R is the additional gross-respiration carbon equivalent. The routine repeats these calculations across NPH × NPT gas substeps, with dts_gas = 1/(NPH × NPT).

Previous aqueous and gaseous CH₄ exchange, plus current methane production by acetoclastic and hydrogenotrophic methanogens, are distributed over these substeps. Gas–water exchange uses solubility, air and water volumes, and DiffusivitySolutEff; the exchange is bounded by the donor pool. For surface litter, gaseous CH₄ is initialized from atmospheric concentration and the rate is also capped by that assigned gaseous amount.

The resulting potential oxygen demand is:

O2 demand = 5.333 × P + 2.667 × R
fLimO2Autor = min(1, max(0, actual O2 uptake / O2 demand))

The coefficients are g O₂ per g C. AerobicAutorO2Uptake solves diffusion/uptake and gas–water exchange over substeps, bounds uptake by available dissolved O₂, then scales P and R by the oxygen limiter. A limiter of 1 means no oxygen restriction; a value near zero means strong restriction. The routine has conditional bypasses, including its surface-litter branch. These equations describe the coded oxygen budget, not an independent validation of complete biochemical stoichiometry.

The corrected cap reserves oxidation, respiration, and retained biomass together. It subtracts previously accumulated respiration from the maintenance allowance, so that hourly maintenance is counted only once across substeps. Later oxygen limitation can reduce final demand; the pre-oxygen reservation is therefore conservative.

Why the cap has two branches

With s=ECHO×η and b=1/η−1, the methane required by a candidate direct-oxidation increment p is:

CH4 demand(p) = (1+s) × p + b × max(0, s×p − Mremaining)

If the increment cannot exhaust the remaining maintenance demand, no new biomass is retained and the cap is H/(1+s). Otherwise, solving the complete budget gives (H+b×Mremaining)/(1+s+b×s). The final rate is the smaller of this supply cap and kinetic capacity.

RGOMP accumulates respiration from preceding gas substeps. Subtracting it from hourly maintenance makes the sum of substep growth reservations equal to max(0,Rtotal−M)×b. No substep receives a fresh hourly maintenance allowance. The factor 0.9999 retains the existing small donor-pool margin. New reservation calculations use MAX(0,...) so small positive carbon requirements are not discarded by the thresholding in AZMAX1.

Sources: AeroMethanotrophCatabolism; AerobicAutorO2Uptake.

4. Anaerobic oxidation links methane to nitrogen cycling

Both anaerobic routines use a decreasing oxygen-inhibition factor, rather than obtaining energy from the aerobic O₂-uptake solver:

OXYI = 1 − 1 / {1 + exp[max(−COXYS + 2.5, −50)]}
fCH4 = CCH4S / (CCH4S + CCK4)

The code uses its aqueous O₂ concentration convention directly in this empirical response. Higher COXYS suppresses these pathways. Both calculate acceptor uptake separately in fertilizer-band and nonband soil, with concentration saturation, kinetic capacity, and competition-weighted donor limits.

NC10: nitrite becomes N₂

CH4 + (8/3) NO2⁻ + (8/3) H⁺ → CO2 + (4/3) N2 + (10/3) H2O
NO2-N consumed / CH4-C oxidized ≈ 3.111

Capacity also contains a CO₂ saturation factor CCO2S/(CCO2S+CCKM), reflecting the model’s external-CO₂ carbon source. Nitrite uptake is capped by the allocated NO₂⁻ pools, then both band and nonband rates are scaled together if total reduction would require more than CH4S × FCH4X. CH₄ oxidation and gross respiration are set to total nitrite-N reduction divided by 3.111.

The biomass update assigns RCO2XumpAutor = G: biomass carbon is withdrawn from environmental CO₂. Catabolism releases CO₂ equal to the oxidized methane carbon, and aggregation routes the reduced nitrite nitrogen to N₂. This pathway does not use the nitrifier NO₂⁻-to-N₂O branch.

ANME-2d: nitrate becomes nitrite

CH4 + 4 NO3⁻ → 4 NO2⁻ + HCO3⁻ + H2O + H⁺
NO3-N consumed / CH4-C oxidized ≈ 4.667

Nitrate-dependent capacity is additionally attenuated by 1/[1+VMXDXT/(VMKI×VOLWZ)], labeled product inhibition in the code. It uses potential rate per water volume rather than an explicit product concentration. After nitrate donor caps, the two compartment rates are scaled together if their methane requirement exceeds the allocated CH₄ pool.

The code initially produces CO₂ equal to CH₄ oxidation, then subtracts biomass growth G from that CO₂ production. This represents reassimilation of oxidation-derived carbon. Nitrate consumption produces nitrite for the wider nitrogen cycle; this routine does not itself reduce that nitrogen all the way to N₂.

For both anaerobic groups, ECHZAutor varies with a methane-concentration-dependent energy expression and is bounded between EO2X and 1. ANME-2d and NC10 therefore do not simply inherit the aerobic constant EH4X.

Sources: AMONC10Catabolism, AMOANME2dCatabolism; AutotrophAnabolicUpdate; AggregateTransfOMBioResdue.

5. Maintenance, biomass growth, and nutrient supply

StageAutotroph sets the growth environmental response from temperature and exp(0.2×PSISoilMatricP). Maintenance uses kinetic and active structural biomass N, the rate RMOM, a separate temperature response, density modifiers RMOMK, and a pH multiplier 1+max[0,0.25(6.5−pH)].

For these methanotroph pathways, with nitrogen-fixation and additional denitrification respiration absent, the shared calculation simplifies to:

M = maintenance demand; R = realized gross respiration; η = ECHZAutor
Growth respiration = max(0, R − M)
Maintenance deficit = max(0, M − R)
D = min(M, R) + max(0, R − M)/η
G = D − R = max(0, R − M) × (1/η − 1)

D is stored as DOMuptk4GrothAutor(ielmc,NGL). Its carbon-source interpretation depends on the functional group. G enters reserve C; shared allocation then transfers reserve C/N/P into kinetic and structural biomass. Mineral N and phosphate exchange supply or release nutrients according to reserve stoichiometry and donor availability. The nutrient-status multiplier also affects potential catabolism.

When respiration cannot meet maintenance, shared starvation calculations withdraw biomass; ordinary mortality and starvation share bounded donor pools. These transfers and their recycling, residue, humus, and CO₂ products must be included in a whole-step balance. The simpler balances below isolate metabolism and growth.

Sources: StageAutotroph, CalcRespMaintAutor; GatherAutotrophRespiration, GatherAutotrophAnabolicFlux, BiomNutMinerMobilAutor, AutotrophAnabolicUpdate.

7. Carbon accounting: corrected balance and earlier mismatch

Let P denote direct aerobic oxidation, R gross respiration, D carbon entering the biomass/respiration pathway, and G=D−R retained growth. Ignore other organisms, mortality, and methane production for this comparison.

PathwayCH₄-C uptakeNet metabolic CO₂-C releaseBiomass-C gain
Aerobic, complete budgetP+DP+RG=D−R
NC10RR−G (release R, environmental uptake G)G
ANME-2dRR−G (reassimilation)G
Correction applied on 29 September 2026. The API now exports micflx%RCH4UptkAutor, the complete net methane uptake including aerobic respiration and biomass demand. The aerobic substep cap and debit now reserve P+R+G, using the remaining hourly maintenance demand. Oxidation diagnostics remain separate from total uptake.

What the API now sends to transport

trcs_RMicbUptake_vr(idg_CH4,L,NY,NX) = micflx%RCH4UptkAutor

Net CH4 uptake = Σaerobic(P + D) + Σanaerobic(CH4 oxidation)
                − total microbial CH4 production

Positive flux means methane consumption; negative flux means net production. The complete net flux is assembled in AggregateTransfOMBioResdue. The API now uses that value directly. RCH4Oxi_aero_vr continues to report direct oxidation P; it should not be interpreted as the full aerobic methane sink.

Earlier behavior: aggregation calculated complete uptake P+D, but the API exported an aerobic contribution of only P. CO₂ plus biomass outputs totaled P+D, leaving D without a CH₄ withdrawal. Separately, the internal donor calculation reserved only P+R, omitting biomass carbon G.

A source-extracted reproduction of the earlier code, including the O₂ solver, produced total demand of 0.001449 from 0.001 available CH₄-C. That is a historical controlled reproduction, not an expected error in the corrected code or a claim about every simulation.

The correction preserves the existing oxygen-demand formulation and lagged competition shares. It closes these two identified carbon-budget gaps; it does not establish that every aspect of oxygen stoichiometry or ecosystem competition is scientifically validated.

Relevant sites: CH₄ cap and debit in AeroMethanotrophCatabolism; complete uptake at ~2205, oxidation diagnostic at ~2318; complete CH₄ export in MicBGCAPI; consumer of the exported gas sink.
