Kinetic components of solid organic matter

Code-based knowledge note · 18 September 2026 · Sources refer to this repository checkout.

EcoSIM subdivides each solid organic matter complex into kinetic components, whose meanings depend on the complex. Litter uses four biochemical fractions; POM uses one kinetic fraction; humus uses two resistance classes. See also microbial complexes and layer coverage.

Composition, nutrients, and colonization

SolidOM(element,M,K) holds C, N, and P separately for each component and complex. SolidOMAct(M,K) holds colonized/active carbon. Initial carbon fractions are specified by CFOSC_vr, with litter composition varying by residue type and component nutrient ratios represented by CNOSC_vr and CPOSC_vr.

POM is initialized with component fractions (1,0,0,0); humus uses (FC1,1−FC1,0,0). The humus fraction FC1 = FC0 × FCX depends on nutrient-to-carbon ratios, accumulated overlying SOC, and natural versus reconstructed soil settings. Sources: InitSOMBGCMod.F90, lines 346–360, 462–566, and 594–658.

Colonization increases active carbon according to microbial activity and the complex-specific coefficient DOSA(K), capped by total component carbon. Source: MicrobialLitterColonization in MicBGCFGMod.F90, lines 1899–1923.

The declaration labels SPOSC as h⁻¹, but these values are not standalone first-order turnover rates: the hydrolysis equation combines them with a microbial-activity scalar and a cap. Their reciprocals should not be interpreted as pool residence times. Source: MicBGCPars.F90, line 41 and lines 329–335.

How the components decompose

For each component M in complex K, carbon hydrolysis follows this implementation (notation shortened):

activity_scalar(K) = max(0, A(K)) × DFNS(K) × OQCI(K) / BulkSOMC(K)
H_C(M,K) = SolidOMAct(M,K) × min(0.5, SPOSC(M,K) × activity_scalar(K))
  • A(K) is ROQC4HeterMicActCmpK(K), respiration of DOC plus acetate used to represent microbial activity.
  • DFNS = COSC / (COSC + DCKD) expresses substrate-concentration saturation; DCKD increases with aqueous microbial activity and uses different base constants for surface litter and soil.
  • OQCI = 1 / (1 + DOC_concentration / OQKI) represents inhibition by accumulated DOC.
  • The calculation acts on colonized carbon and caps hydrolysis at 50% of that active carbon per calculation. Elemental hydrolysis is additionally capped by available mass.

N and P hydrolysis uses the fraction of total component carbon hydrolyzed, divided by the respective microbial nutrient limitation factors FCNK and FCPK (bounded between 0.5 and 1), and capped by available N or P. Thus nutrient release need not remain exactly proportional to carbon release. Environmental effects also enter through microbial activity; the carbon equation shown does not directly multiply by a separate temperature factor.

Source: SolidOMDecomposition in MicBGCFGMod.F90, lines 1305–1375.
