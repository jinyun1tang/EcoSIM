Root categories, lengths, axes, and porosity

How a representative primary axis becomes a plant’s root system—and how the model connects coarse roots, optional medium roots, fine roots, and mycorrhizae.

1. Two classification systems overlap

Primary and secondary describe the model’s root-axis organization. Coarse, medium, and fine describe functional compartments. A primary axis can retain a fine, absorptive portion near its growing end while an older portion undergoes secondary growth and becomes coarse. “Primary root” therefore does not always mean “coarse root.”

Medium roots are optional and active only for trees when lignification is considered. They represent an intermediate branching and transport compartment between coarse structural roots and some fine roots. Other fine roots connect directly to the primary-root system; medium roots are not an obligatory stage for every root.

Exact implementation switches for medium roots

GrowRootMycoAxes calls GrowMediumRootAxes when lcoarseroot is true, is_plant_woody_vascular is true, primary length in that layer is positive, and N=ipltroot. The woody predicate is iPlantRootProfile_pft > 1 and iPlant2ndGrothPattern_pft == 1; the current tree-like code is 2.

The modeling condition above associates medium roots with tree lignification. In the current call site, lcoarseroot is the explicit switch; llignification is a separate control used in lignification/thickening calculations and is not directly tested at this call. Nonzero growth also depends on carbon supply and allocation.

2. Lengths and counts: keep the accounting basis explicit

NZ identifies the PFT, L the soil layer, NR the structural-root group, and N the plant-root or mycorrhizal category. A group can represent multiple similar primary axes; it is not necessarily one literal root. Effective axis counts can be fractional.

Let P be PlantPopuLive_pft(NZ), S be NumStructuralRootAxes_pft(NZ), and a be Num1stAxesPerStructRootX_pft(NZ). In the native model, initialization multiplies planting density by grid-cell area to obtain P. “Population total” below means the modeled population in that cell. Divide a population total by cell area when an areal density is required.

3. Explore the primary-axis scaling

For equal representative lengths in every group, per-plant length is S × a × ℓ, and population length is P × S × a × ℓ. Each multiplier appears once. The controls illustrate this identity; they are not an EcoSIM simulation.

2primary axes per plant
0.2 mprimary length per plant
0.6 mprimary length across population

Two groups × one axis × 0.1 m = 0.2 m per plant; three plants give 0.6 m.

The corrected DiagRootGeometry sums Root1stLenPP_rpvr × Num1stAxesPerStructRootX_pft within the NR loop. This applies to coarse-primary length, fine-primary length, and fine-primary absorptive volume.

4. From length and radius to uptake geometry

Fine-root extent

Total fine length controls the extent of absorptive tissue. Effective axis count controls how that length is distributed among parallel branches.

mean fine-axis length = L₂ / n₂

Cylinder geometry

For radius r and compatible total length L, lateral surface and volume scale as:

A = 2πrL
V = πr²L

Coarse-root thickness

Structural carbon and volume per carbon determine coarse volume. Length and axis count then constrain the inferred radius.

r ≈ √[V / (πL)]

What the layer diagnostics include

In DiagRootGeometry, let L1c and L1f be coarse and fine primary lengths per plant, and L2,pop the population fine-secondary length for category N.

RootLenPerPlant_pvr = L1c + L1f
RootTotLenPerPlant_pvr = L1c + L1f + L2,pop/P
RootAbsorbLenPerPlant_pvr = L1f + L2,pop/P

For mycorrhizae, this routine does not add a plant primary-axis length. Also, these named diagnostics do not add the separate medium-root length. If you need an inventory of all represented plant-root length, account for medium roots separately rather than treating RootTotLenPerPlant_pvr as that inventory.

RootLenDensPerPlant_pvr is calculated here as per-plant total length divided by layer thickness DLYR3. A conventional root-length density per soil volume requires population length divided by actual soil volume; the variable name alone does not establish that quantity.

Axis count is not interchangeable with length

The fine-axis growth calculation combines branching potential with an upper bound Root2ndLen_rpvr / Root2ndTipLen4uptk. The resulting effective count is limited by available root length. Layer mean effective length is calculated from total fine length / count, with a minimum tip length. The count enters parallel axial conductance and distributes layer water uptake among structural groups.

When medium roots exist, the branching support length includes RootMediumLength_rpvr + DLYR3 × P. Both terms are on a population basis. Without medium roots, the branching calculation uses layer thickness and the population primary-axis count. This allows direct fine-root support as well as medium-root-supported fine roots.

Sources: RootMod.F90, Grow2ndRootAxes, DiagRootGeometry, CytoKininDynamics; UptakesMod.F90, CalcPlantHydroResistance.

5. Root porosity and xylem lumen describe different spaces

RootPorosity_pft(N,NZ) is a dimensionless root air-space fraction, φ. It is separate from soil porosity. The geometry routine partitions the effective absorptive-root volume into air space and its modeled water-occupied complement:

Vair = φ Vabs → RootPoreVol_pvr
Vwater = (1 − φ) Vabs → RootVH2O_pvr
Vair + Vwater = Vabs

For example, Vabs = 1 cm³ and φ = 0.25 give 0.25 cm³ air space and 0.75 cm³ modeled water volume. This is an effective compartment partition. It is not a separate calculation of solid tissue volume and liquid saturation. Mycorrhizae use the same category-specific formulation; their initial porosity is copied from the host root porosity.

For each fine-root group, Root2ndVH2O_rpvr is calculated directly as Root2ndXSecArea_pft × Root2ndLen_rpvr × (1 − φ). The layer-level Vabs can also include biomass- and turgor-based lower bounds and different treatment of woody primary portions. It therefore need not equal a simple sum of all compartment cylinder volumes.

Xylem lumen is the conducting water space

The xylem lumen fraction is diagnosed separately from radius and the xylemPhi_min_pft/xylemPhi_max_pft parameters. It converts conducting sapwood area into open lumen area for sap flow and cytokinin transport. It should not be replaced by either φ or 1 − φ.

6. Geometry must follow surviving roots

Growth updates structural biomass, lengths, and branching counts. Withdrawal and death can shorten or remove roots. The final geometry refresh then rebuilds fine-root totals and volumes for all active categories and layers before cytokinin dynamics.

For a fine-root group with negligible structural carbon or nonpositive length, the refreshed axis count is zero. For surviving roots, the count cannot exceed length divided by minimum tip length, and this refresh cannot create new axes. If a primary axis retreats into an upper layer, associated fine-root biomass, length, and counts must move together.

Cytokinin is represented as concentration. Retaining concentration while living root volume decreases implies a proportional reduction in cytokinin mass. Zero living volume requires a zero concentration state; the full root-death pathway also clears fine-, medium-, and coarse-root cytokinin concentrations. Root geometry and chemical concentrations must therefore be interpreted together.

Sources: RootMod.F90, DiagRootGeometry, PrimeRootsWithdraw, CytoKininDynamics; LitterFallMod.F90, LiterFallDeadRoots.
