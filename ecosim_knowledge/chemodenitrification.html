Chemical denitrification

EcoSIM converts nitrite into nitrate, N₂O, and dissolved organic nitrogen through a pH-sensitive chemical pathway. Its rate depends on nitrite concentration, water volume, temperature, and competition for nitrite. It does not require an active microbial biomass pool.

Implementation snapshot · 29 September 2026 · solphys · HEAD 3db3ff14 plus the uncommitted chemical-denitrification DON conservation fix. Primary source: ChemoDenitrification and AggregateTransfOMBioResdue.

1. One nitrite sink, three nitrogen products

For each unit of nitrite-N consumed, the implemented product fractions are 80% nitrate-N, 10% N₂O-N, and 10% DON-N. Fertilizer-band and nonband nitrite are processed separately; the DON products are then combined for the layer.

80% NO₃-N10%
N₂O-N10%
DON
MINERAL PRODUCT

Nitrate

Returns to the same band or nonband mineral compartment that supplied nitrite.

GAS PRODUCT

Nitrous oxide

Enters the layer gas-exchange accounting. Production is a negative contribution to net N₂O uptake.

ORGANIC PRODUCT

Dissolved organic N

Enters the active organic complexes and is counted as inorganic-to-organic nitrogen transfer.

All fractions are on an N-mass basis. A flux of 0.1 g N as N₂O is not 0.1 g of N₂O molecules. This routine produces no N₂, consumes no NH₄, and specifies no microbial growth or maintenance yield.

The name “chemical denitrification” refers here to the implemented nitrous-acid-dependent transformation. Most consumed nitrogen returns as nitrate. The 80/10/10 split is an imposed product allocation, not a mechanistic reaction network tracking all atoms, electrons, or intermediates.

2. pH, nitrite, water, and temperature set potential demand

Let j denote nonband soil (s) or fertilizer band (b). The routine uses nitrite-N concentration Cj, nitrite-N pool Nj, compartment fraction fj, micropore water volume W, and the common temperature multiplier T.

h = max(ZERO, 10^(3 − pH))
HNO₂ proxyj = Cj × h / 0.5
Vj = 0.075 × HNO₂ proxyj × W × fj × T
Rj = max(0, min(Nj × Fj, Vj))

Cs = CNO2S              Cb = CNO2B
Ns = ZNO2S              Nb = ZNO2B
fs = VLNO3              fb = VLNOB
W  = VLWatMicPM(NPH)    T  = TSensGrowth

Vj is potential nitrite consumption before the competition-allocated donor cap. Rj is realized consumption. Concentration and volume supply the model's layer mass basis; the resulting fluxes are hourly N amounts on that spatial basis. The coefficient 0.075 acts as an hourly rate factor.

Before a donor cap or numerical floor intervenes, lowering pH by one unit multiplies the potential rate by ten. The variables CHNO2/CHNOB are the code's nitrous-acid concentration proxies. The expression is not a bounded acid-fraction equilibrium calculation; h/0.5 can exceed one. Interpret it as the implemented kinetic parameterization.

The temperature factor is calculated by MicrobPhysTempFun and shared with microbial growth. Its use here does not introduce a biomass dependency. This routine applies no separate oxygen limiter, redox gate, DOC/acetate donor requirement, Fe(II) dependence, or microbial nutrient-stress factor. Its direct water dependence is through the supplied concentration and VLWatMicPM(NPH).

Sources: ChemoDenitrification and StageBGCEnvironCondition; MicrobPhysTempFun.

3. The donor share comes from previous-hour demand

Each compartment has its own competition factor. Chemical demand competes with other nitrite-consuming pathways through ecosystem demand totals.

If previous ecosystem NO₂ demandj > ZEROS:
  Fj = max(FMN, previous chemical demandj / previous ecosystem demandj)
Otherwise:
  Fj = FMN × fj

FMN = 0.005 in the parameter initialization
Current chemical demand saved for the next hour = Vj

The saved chemical demand is the potential rate, not the realized, donor-limited rate. RedistMod includes it in ecosystem nitrite-demand totals, which are carried into the next hour. The API imports the previous chemical demand before the layer calculation.

The fallback includes the band/nonband fraction; the ratio branch applies the minimum share directly. The factor is not explicitly clipped at one or renormalized across consumers in this routine. Thus min(Nj × Fj,Vj) is specifically a cap by the allocated donor amount, not an unconditional min(Nj,Vj) cap independent of the supplied competition state.

Sources: MicAPISend and MicAPIRecv; ecosystem demand accumulation; FMN initialization.

4. Closing the nitrogen budget

Write R = Rs + Rb for total chemical nitrite consumption. The current corrected implementation accounts for all products:

ΔNO₂s = −Rs                 ΔNO₂b = −Rb
ΔNO₃s = 0.8 Rs              ΔNO₃b = 0.8 Rb
N₂O-N production = 0.1 R
DON-N production = 0.1 R

Nitrogen balance = −R + 0.8 R + 0.1 R + 0.1 R = 0

What the recent correction changes

RNO2ReduxChemo already calculated 0.1 R, but that value was previously unused. Nitrite was removed in full, while only nitrate and N₂O returned to model pools. For consumption of 0.15 N mass units, the model returned 0.135 and lost 0.015.

The correction adds the missing DON in AggregateTransfOMBioResdue. Because the chemical reaction has no complex-specific donor, its layer-wide DON is distributed equally among the KL active organic complexes:

dDON = RNO2ReduxChemo / KL
DOM(idom_don, 1:KL)         += dDON
REcoDOMProd(idom_don, 1:KL) += dDON
TRDOM2DIE(ielmn)            += RNO2ReduxChemo

KL is the number of litter complexes for surface litter and the full complex count for soil. Litter therefore receives no DON allocation to inactive POM or humus complexes. Equal allocation is a bookkeeping choice for this unresolved complex association, not an inferred preference for particular organic substrates.

The three updates have distinct roles: DOM changes the actual dissolved pool; REcoDOMProd reports its production flux; and the N component of TRDOM2DIE records inorganic-to-organic transfer. They describe one transfer, not three nitrogen inputs. MicAPIRecv exports the updated DOM state and its flux separately.

No extra DOC or phosphate is created by this nitrogen-only update. The correction retains the existing product fractions and kinetics. It does not add a chemical mechanism for how nitrite-N is incorporated into organic matter.

Sources: AggregateTransfOMBioResdue; pool, flux, and transfer-accounting export.

5. Where it runs in the hourly layer calculation

StageLoad pools, concentrations and prior demand; calculate environmental factors.
ReactAfter microbial catabolism, calculate chemical nitrite consumption and its three products.
AggregateAfter decomposition and microbial biomass updates, add mineral/gas products and credit DON.
ExportReturn the updated organic pools, reaction fluxes and next-hour competition demand.

SoilBGCOneLayer calls ChemoDenitrification once per active layer calculation, outside any microbial guild loop. The same routine can operate for litter and soil when the caller admits the layer. It uses the inputs prepared for that call; it is not a second equilibrium solve over newly produced nitrite.

With the current correction, chemical DON is added during final aggregation, after this call's decomposition, sorption, and microbial uptake calculations. It therefore becomes available to those processes on a subsequent call. Mineral and gas effects are exported through their usual flux interfaces.

Source: SoilBGCOneLayer execution order.

8. Interpretation and source map

This pathway differs from microbial denitrification: it has no guild-specific biomass, respiration, or maintenance calculation. It also differs from nitrifier denitrification, which couples nitrite reduction with additional NH₄ consumption. Here the only explicitly debited nitrogen donor is nitrite.

The source does not resolve NO as an intermediate or product, Fe-mediated chemistry, or an electron donor for the DON branch. The pH response and fixed yields should be understood as this model's representation, not a general chemical stoichiometry. Correcting the missing DON closes the implemented nitrogen balance without establishing the physical validity of those assumptions.
