How vegetation, snow, litter, and soil exchange sensible heat and water vapor—and how those exchanges feed the diagnosed canopy-air temperature Tq and vapor concentration VPQ.

VPQ is not relative humidity. Although some comments say “vapor pressure,” these exchange equations multiply concentration differences by volume conductances. The code uses water-equivalent vapor concentration; interpreting it as pressure in Pa would give incorrect dimensions.

Below, H, LE, and vapor transfer F are positive from air into the receiving surface or vegetation. Evaporation/transpiration gives F < 0 and LE < 0. Positive outward evapotranspiration is E = −F. H denotes an hourly heat rate or the numerically equivalent heat integrated over the model’s one-hour vegetation step; ground substeps explicitly integrate their own Δt.

2 · Tq and the sensible-heat resistance paths

Executed exchange pathLagged feedback / resistance weighting

Pan the schematic horizontally on a narrow screen. Each solid branch represents the resistance used to evaluate a heat flux; it is not a claim that every branch is solved as one simultaneous electrical-style network. Plant sensible heat uses ambient Tₐ directly. Ground heat uses Tq. The dashed loop assembles the feedback for the next hour.

Hₚ = c_air Aₚ(Tₐ − T꜀,p)/rₚ
H_j = c_air A_H,j(Tq − T_j)/r_j,   j ∈ {soil, litter, snow}

HeatXAir2PCan_pft is Hₚ. Ground terms are HeatSensAir2Grnd, HeatSensAir2LitR, and HeatSensAir2Snow, accumulated and corrected by the implicit surface solves. r has units h m⁻¹; c_air = 1.25 × 10⁻³ MJ m⁻³ K⁻¹; A is m². Thus c_air A/r is an hourly heat conductance [MJ h⁻¹ K⁻¹]. For a ground substep, its integrated coefficient additionally includes Δt.

The weighting resistance is not necessarily the exchange resistance. The physical heat flux is calculated using r_j or rₚ. Later, that accepted heat is multiplied by w_g or wₚ to estimate its effect on canopy air. Multiplying by that weight does not create another heat transfer.

4 · VPQ and the vapor-resistance paths

Vapor exchangeLatent-heat feedback

Leaf, canopy-surface, soil, litter, and snow exchange all contribute to atmospheric water/latent heat. Plant vapor transport uses ambient ρₐ; ground vapor transport uses diagnosed ρq. Intercepted-water evaporation and transpiration are allocated sequentially in the plant algorithm, rather than independently exhausting the same vapor demand.

F_j,potential = A_v,j(ρq − ρ_j)/(r_j + RZ)
ρ_g = vapsat(T_g) exp[18 Ψ_g/(RGASC T_g)]
ρ_l = vapsat(T_l) exp[18 Ψ_l/(RGASC T_l)]
ρ_s = vapsat(T_s)

For soil, Ψ_g is matric plus osmotic potential; the litter expression uses its calculated matric potential. These exponential forms follow the source’s MPa/kelvin/RGASC convention. For a ground substep, include Δt in the conductance. Proposed evaporation is bounded by the available donor water; snow draws liquid first and then dry-snow water equivalent.

Low VPQ increases evaporation demand. As soil/litter dries, its water potential suppresses surface vapor concentration and available water limits the accepted loss. Warming increases vapsat; evaporative latent cooling then feeds back on surface temperature. Aerodynamic resistance and litter pore resistance also change with thermal stability and water/air-filled porosity.

Sources: SurfPhysMod.F90 (SoilSRFEnerbyBalanceM); SurfLitterPhysMod.F90 (SurfLitterIterationM); SnowPhysMod.F90 (SnowAtmosExchangeMM).

5 · Energy balance: aerodynamic resistance is one part

Δ(CT) = Q_rad + Q_H + Q_LE + Q_water enthalpy + Q_conduction + Q_phase + Q_other
Q_H = c_air A_H Δt (T_air boundary − T_surface)/r_a
Q_LE = L_v F_liquid + L_s F_sublimation

All Q terms in this storage equation are integrated heat [MJ per cell/step], and F denotes the corresponding signed step water volume. C is heat capacity [MJ K⁻¹]; it changes with liquid/ice content, so Δ(CT) is generally not CΔT. Internal paired transfers cancel only when both sides use the same accepted water/heat.

Radiative forcing

Q_rad = absorbed SW + incoming LW − emitted LW
LW_ground,in = (LW_sky f_transmission + LW_canopy,previous hour) Δt

Canopy shading changes absorbed shortwave. Previous-hour canopy emission contributes to ground incoming longwave. Ground outgoing longwave, including accepted temperature corrections, is passed to the plant calculation later in the same hour.

Mass and internal exchange

Q_adv = c_water T_donor F_water

Rain, snowmelt drainage, infiltration, root water uptake/release, and runoff carry sensible enthalpy. Vapor transfer also has a mass-enthalpy term, distinct from latent heat. Litter–soil conduction and vapor exchange transfer heat internally.

Ground litter/snow and topsoil temperature solves retain derivative terms to reduce abrupt changes. Their accepted corrections are recorded in heat and radiation/sensible totals. These stabilize surface temperature response; they do not by themselves stabilize the separate hourly Tq/VPQ diagnostics.

For vegetation, net radiation includes absorbed shortwave and sky/ground longwave minus two-sided canopy emission. Temperature and water potential are iterated with stomatal resistance, root-water supply, and tissue storage. The vegetation temperature candidate is limited to ambient ±5 K and then under-relaxed; this is separate from the diagnosed Tq used by ground physics.

Detailed equations: canopy energy balance and ground-surface energy balance.

6 · What contributes to evapotranspiration?

Using positive outward water loss, the component sum is schematically

ET = E_transpiration + E_intercepted liquid + E_canopy snow + E_soil + E_litter + E_ground snow

Condensation/dew contributes negative loss under this outward convention. Each component must use its effective area, own substep duration, and donor limits before being combined. Dry snow loss is expressed as water equivalent, not bulk snow volume.

Transpiration: aerodynamic and stomatal resistance in series

E_transpiration,dry = Aₚ(ρ_leaf − ρₐ)/(rₚ + r_stomata)
ρ_leaf = vapsat(T꜀) exp[18 max(Ψ꜀, −5000)/(RGASC T꜀)]
r_stomata = r_min + (r_cuticle − r_min) exp(−Ψ_turgor/RCS)

These dry-canopy equations show how temperature, water potential, atmospheric humidity, and stomatal closure set transpiration demand. Lower turgor increases resistance. The plant uses VPA, mapped from ambient VPA_col; substituting VPQ would change the implemented coupling.

Wet canopy: allocate vapor demand before transpiration

EX₀ = (Aₚ/rₚ)(ρₐ − ρ_leaf)
F_surface,potential = EX₀ rₚ/(rₚ + ResistLeafSurf)
EX_remaining = EX₀ − (F_intercepted liquid + F_canopy snow)
F_transpiration = EX_remaining rₚ/(rₚ + r_stomata)

During evaporation, potential surface loss first draws intercepted liquid, then intercepted snow subject to availability. The current source subtracts their combined accepted loss, VapXAir2Canopy_pft, from EX before the stomatal factor is applied. With condensation, dew is assigned to liquid gain and residual transpiration demand is set to zero. These statements follow the current code; the older standalone transpiration note predates the combined liquid-plus-snow subtraction.

Supply and storage complete the calculation

ΔW_tissue = F_transpiration − q_root
q_root,potential = (Ψ꜀ + Ψ_gravity − Ψ_soil)/R_soil+root

Here both transpiration and root uptake have their code signs: transpiration < 0, uptake from soil < 0. Water potential is adjusted so net root supply minus transpiration approximately matches tissue-storage change. Hydraulic release back to soil is allowed, with availability/space limits. Roots therefore connect ET to soil water, while stored tissue water can temporarily support demand.

References: transpiration, canopy water potential, Ci/Ca–transpiration feedback; current implementation: UptakesMod.F90.

7 · From accepted fluxes to Tq and VPQ

Assemble resistance-weighted heat, not just total heat

S_H = w_g H_ground + Σₚ wₚ Hₚ
S_L = w_g LE_ground + Σₚ wₚ LEₚ

RootUptakes adds plant terms to Air_Heat_Sens_store_col and Air_Heat_Latent_store_col. After the ground and plant updates, REDIST adds the combined surface terms. At the next HOUR1, these accumulators are copied into TSHX_col and TLEX_col, then cleared for the new hour.

The explicit diagnostic forms

Tq_new = Tₐ,new − S_H,completed hour/(c_air A)
VPQ_new = ρₐ,new − S_L,completed hour/(L_v A)

H is positive into a surface: negative S_H represents heating of air, so the explicit Tq increases above ambient. LE is also positive into a surface: evaporation gives negative S_L, so VPQ increases above ambient. Positive condensation removes atmospheric water and decreases VPQ in this expression.

Latent feedback is not exactly a weighted liquid-volume sum when snow is involved. Sublimation contributes L_s F_snow, but the VPQ diagnostic divides the total by L_v. The resulting water-equivalent feedback includes an L_s/L_v factor on snow sublimation. This is the implemented latent-energy-to-vapor diagnostic.

VPQ is not recomputed by imposing saturation at Tq. The current SetCanopyProperty expression does not itself enforce zero humidity or a saturation bound. Tq affects vapor exchange through temperature-dependent saturation, resistance, and evolving surface states; VPQ affects latent cooling through accepted vapor exchange. These are coupled dynamically even though their diagnostic formulas are separate.

What a fixed-condition resistance closure would mean

Freeze ground temperatures, areas, resistances, and the plant sensible-heat contribution S_H,plant. Define g_j = c_air A_H,j/r_j. The ground part then has the affine dependence

S_H(Tq) = S_H,plant + w_g Σ_j g_j(Tq − T_j)
K_H,frozen = ∂S_H/∂Tq = w_g Σ_j g_j
Tq_fixed = [c_air A Tₐ − S_H,plant + w_g Σ_j g_j T_j]/[c_air A + w_g Σ_j g_j]

This is a mathematical frozen-condition closure, not the model’s complete same-hour solution. Actual surface temperatures, conductances, donor limits, and stabilizer corrections respond during the hour. Plant heat directly uses Tₐ, so its conductance should not be added as a direct Tq derivative. Ground changes can still affect plants indirectly through radiation and soil conditions.

Sources: UptakesMod.F90, RedistMod.F90, Hour1Mod.F90, SurfPhysMod.F90.

Computing an updated Tq or VPQ at the end of the hour cannot change exchanges already accepted during that hour. It can provide a predictor for the next hour. A genuinely same-hour common-air-node solve would require trial evaluations of the responding components before committing their state and flux changes.

Root water and its enthalpy follow a separate delayed path: the plant update occurs after the current soil solve, so the next hour stages these root transfers into soil substeps. Transpiration itself is an atmospheric plant-water loss, not a direct withdrawal from surface litter.

9 · Resistance-based Tq and VPQ predictors

The hourly secant experiment has been replaced by a resistance estimate. Each active atmospheric ground exchange accumulates its actual step-integrated sensible conductance, including area fractions and inner snow/litter timestep factors:

Ghour = Σactive steps,j cairAexchange,jΔtj/ra,j
K_H = w_g Ghour
Tqⁿ⁺¹ = [C_A Tₐⁿ⁺¹ − S_Hⁿ + K_Hⁿ Tqⁿ]/[C_A + K_Hⁿ],   C_A = c_air A

SurfSensHeatConduct_col is reset after hourly SetCanopyProperty, then rebuilt by soil, litter, and snow exchanges. RedistMod::UpdateCanopyAirDerivatives applies the same w_g used to weight accepted ground sensible heat. The estimate is nonnegative because the active conductances are positive, not because an empirical slope is clipped. It needs no change in Tq between hours.

This derivative holds surface temperatures and resistances fixed. It does not propagate implicit temperature, phase, or indirect plant responses. Plant sensible heat directly uses ambient Tₐ, so its conductance does not enter K_H; its complete heat contribution remains in S_H. The predictor preserves the existing calling order and does not revise completed heat transfers.

The weighted derivative and matching reference Tq are saved in restarts; the within-hour conductance sum is rebuilt. Zero conductance recovers the explicit predictor. VPQ now has an analogous scalar resistance correction. Its weighted latent-energy derivative and matching vapor reference are stored as dLatHeatdVPQ_col and VPQLatHeatRef_col. Missing legacy fields recover the explicit first predictor.

Why the earlier hourly secant was replaced

The signed secant measured simultaneous changes in weather, surface temperature, resistance, water availability, and plant heat, rather than a derivative with other quantities fixed. In the Blodgett scratch test, a slope of −0.001478 against C_A = 0.001250 produced a negative denominator and Tq = 65.75 K on day 8. Five cases in the 15-case smoke screen stopped early. Those results refer to the superseded secant experiment.

A fully coupled heat–vapor closure would involve two residuals

R_T = C_A(Tq − Tₐ) + S_H(Tq,ρq) = 0
R_v = L_v A(ρq − ρₐ) + S_L(Tq,ρq) = 0

Tunable scalar predictors

x_new = [x_a − (F − D x_old)/C]/[1 + D/C]
D = cfeedback × K,   K = K_H or K_V
C = c_air A for Tq;   C = L_v A for VPQ

This is the explicit solution of x_new = x_a − [F + D(x_new − x_old)]/C, with F and x_old from the same completed hour. The compile-time cfeedback parameter is shared by temperature and humidity: 0 disables stabilization, intermediate values weaken it, and 1 applies the full estimate. Scaling is local to the predictor; restart-persisted derivatives are unchanged.

K_V = w_g Σ_j ∂LE_j/∂ρq uses the accepted atmospheric exchange branches at fixed stores. Soil/litter and liquid-snow exchange contribute L_v Cd when unconstrained; active sublimation contributes L_s Cd; a fully donor-limited branch contributes zero. Conductances already include area and timestep factors. Plant vapor exchange uses ambient VPA, so its full latent heat remains in the forcing but contributes no direct VPQ derivative.

Stability is not a guarantee of unbiased mean conditions. The predictors mix the explicit estimate with the previous-hour reference. This can retain colder or drier conditions during warming or moistening, with opposite lags during cooling or drying. Surface adjustment, accepted implicit heat corrections, and cross-responses are omitted from the direct slope estimate. Nonlinear exchanges can turn these lags into mean changes; the full hourly effective sensitivity is not established by summing conductances.

Separate diagnostics do not imply thermodynamic independence

The current diagnostic treats Tq and ρq (TKQ_col and VPQ_col) as independent unknowns in separate sensible- and latent-heat balances. It imposes no direct saturation relationship between them. Temperature alone does not determine vapor concentration: relative humidity and vapor pressure follow from both variables.

RH = ρq / ρ_sat(Tq)
e_q = ρ_v R_v Tq

In the vapor-pressure identity, ρ_v is vapor mass density in kg m⁻³, obtained by converting the code’s water-equivalent concentration; R_v is the specific gas constant for water vapor. Use a consistent liquid-water or ice saturation reference when interpreting RH. Neither the existing diagnostic nor a separate resistance-based humidity predictor enforces ρq ≤ ρ_sat(Tq). Surface condensation depends on the receiving surface’s equilibrium vapor concentration, so it can occur even when canopy air is unsaturated. Removing supersaturation in the air would require accounting for condensed water and released latent heat.

The scalar approximation freezes cross-responses during the prediction; it does not assume temperature and humidity are physically unrelated. Tq and ρq remain coupled through surface temperatures, saturation concentrations, latent cooling, aerodynamic resistance, and evolving water stores. Separate scalar stabilizers omit the off-diagonal derivatives of those responses.

For a joint linearization about a matching completed-hour reference (T_r, ρ_r), define C_V = L_v A and evaluate the following derivatives at that reference:

(C_A + ∂S_H/∂Tq) ΔTq + (∂S_H/∂ρq) Δρq = C_A(Tₐ,new − T_r) − S_H,r
(∂S_L/∂Tq) ΔTq + (C_V + ∂S_L/∂ρq) Δρq = C_V(ρₐ,new − ρ_r) − S_L,r
Tq_new = T_r + ΔTq,   ρq_new = ρ_r + Δρq

The two off-diagonal terms represent heat–humidity cross-responses. For the simplest direct resistance paths, holding surface temperatures, resistances, water stores, and phase allocation fixed makes these cross-derivatives zero. Allowing the surfaces and their exchange properties to respond generally introduces cross-coupling; obtaining those derivatives requires more than summing direct conductances. Plant exchange currently uses ambient Tₐ and ρₐ, so it contributes no direct Tq or ρq derivative under the implemented coupling.

This joint system is a conceptual framework for future work. The code implements separate scalar resistance-based corrections for Tq and VPQ, with a shared tunable slope multiplier. Their off-diagonal cross-responses remain omitted; the joint heat–humidity solve is not implemented.
