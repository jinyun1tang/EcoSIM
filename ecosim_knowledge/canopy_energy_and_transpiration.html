EcoSIM process walkthrough

Canopy energy, transpiration,
and the ground below

EcoSIM solves vegetation temperature and water potential together: radiation and vapor exchange set water demand, root hydraulics supplies water, and tissue storage closes the balance. The ground and vegetation are advanced sequentially within each hour.

1 · The hourly calling sequence

WATSUB calls StageSurfacePhysModel → SetCanopyProperty once, then loops over NPH heat–water substeps. Each substep evaluates surface exchange before UpdateSoilMoistTempM accepts soil temperature and its implicit heat correction. Before returning, WATSUB copies TKSoil1_vr into TKS_vr.

PlantModel runs afterward. Its plant API receives the updated soil state, the available-water history, and ground longwave emission. RootUptakes then solves canopy temperature and hydraulics. New root transfers cannot be applied retroactively to the ground solve that already finished: their previous-hour copies enter the following WATSUB call.

Two different air boundaries: plant energy and transpiration use ambient TairK_col and VPA_col, mapped into the plant API as TairK and VPA. Ground sensible/vapor exchanges use diagnosed TKQ_col and VPQ_col. These variables should not be treated as interchangeable.

Sources: EcoSIMAPI.F90 (Run_EcoSIM_one_step); WatsubMod.F90 (watsub, InitSoilHydrauics); PlantColumnTransferMod.F90.

The equations below follow the code’s one-hour vegetation update. Hourly rates and their one-hour increments have identical numerical values. In a general timestep formulation, multiply rates and conductances by Δt before adding them to water or heat storage. Ground substeps explicitly use dts_HeatWatTP.

3 · Vegetation energy balance

Radiation and aerodynamic exchange

F = εcan σ fPAR A sOTC
Lemit,one side = F Tc⁴
Rn = SWabs + (LWsky + LWground) fPAR − 2 F Tc⁴
∂Rn/∂Tc = −8 F Tc³

FTHRM is F; FracPARads2Canopy_pft is fPAR; EMS_Modify_Scalar_col is the canopy emissivity modifier. The factor two represents the two emitting sides in this canopy energy expression. One-side emission is accumulated into downward canopy longwave LWRadCanG_col.

GH = cair Aeff / ra,   cair = 1.25 × 10⁻³ MJ m⁻³ K⁻¹
H = GH (Ta − Tc)

The iteration updates aerodynamic resistance from Richardson stability, limiting each resistance update to 0.9–1.1 times its previous iteration value, with a lower resistance bound. These aerodynamic bounds differ from the bounds used for ground surfaces.

Water carries both latent heat and sensible enthalpy

LE = Lv(e + eliq) + Lsesnow
Qprec = (cwPrain + csPsnow) Ta
Qadd = Rn + LE + Qvapor,enthalpy + Qprec − Qroot,out

Evaporation/transpiration and sublimation make LE negative. HeatEvapSens is a separate water-mass enthalpy term: condensation carries cpw × TairK; liquid loss carries cpw × TKC1; snow sublimation carries cps × TKC1. Root uptake has negative CumPlantHeatLoss2Soil, so subtracting it adds imported heat to vegetation. Heat capacities for transported liquid and snow are volumetric, MJ m⁻³ K⁻¹.

The temperature candidate actually used

Let T₀ = TKCX be the start-of-update vegetation temperature, C₀ its initial heat capacity, Cnew the capacity after trial water changes, and D = −dRadNetdT ≥ 0. In one-hour integrated notation, the code computes:

Tcandidate = [(C₀ + D)T₀ + Qadd + GHTa] / [Cnew + GH + D]
Cnew = C₀ + cw(Prain + eliq + e − q) + cs(Psnow + esnow)

This is the implemented candidate, not a claim that a complete nonlinear energy residual is solved exactly. Radiation is evaluated at the trial temperature, while the derivative term in this expression is anchored to T₀. The candidate is clipped to Ta ± 5 K, then the trial vegetation temperature moves partway toward it. The nearby comment mentioning 10 K does not match the ±5 K expression.

The relaxation factor uses XC, initially 0.5, and a base rate of 0.5 or 0.1 depending on heat capacity. Direction reversals halve XC; water-potential updates can reset it. These are internal vegetation-solver controls, separate from the restored litter and snow outer stabilizers.

Source: UptakesMod.F90, CanopyEnergyH2OIter_func (approximately lines 775–960) and UpdatePlantWaterVars (approximately lines 1523–1530).

4 · From vapor gradient to transpiration

ρleaf = vapsat(Tc) exp[18 max(Ψc, −5000) / (RGASC Tc)]
EX₀ = (Aeff/ra)(ρair − ρleaf)
esurface,potential = EX₀ ra / (ra + rleaf surface)

The exponential is reproduced in the code’s MPa, kelvin, and RGASC convention. More negative water potential suppresses leaf vapor concentration; warming increases the saturation concentration. The leaf-surface resistance here is ResistLeafSurf, distinct from stomatal resistance.

Condensation: EX₀ > 0

Water is assigned to dew/liquid gain. The residual demand for transpiration is set to zero. The code records this in QdewCanopy_pft and VapXAir2CanopyLiq_pft.

Water loss: EX₀ ≤ 0

Potential surface loss first evaporates intercepted liquid, limited by WatHeldOnCanopy_pft, then sublimates intercepted snow, limited by SnowOnCanopy_pft. Their accepted losses are subtracted from EX₀ to obtain the remaining transpiration demand.

EXremaining = EX₀ − (eliq + esnow)
e = EXremaining ra / (ra + rs)

For a dry canopy, positive outward transpiration E = −e simplifies to:

E = Aeff(ρleaf − ρair) / (ra + rs)

This dry-canopy formula does not include interception partitioning. The actual wet-canopy branch uses the sequential allocation above. Transpiration and root uptake are coupled through water potential and tissue storage, rather than enforcing transpiration equal to root supply at every trial.

Hydraulic stress closes stomata

rs = rmin + (rcuticle − rmin) exp(−Ψturgor/RCS)
Ψturgor = max(0, Ψc − Ψosmotic)

Lower turgor raises resistance toward the cuticular limit. StomatalDynamics supplies the photosynthesis-related minimum resistance; CalcStomataResist4H2O applies the current turgor response. Thus temperature, vapor demand, water potential, and photosynthesis affect one another.

Sources: UptakesMod.F90 (approximately lines 835–901); PlantMathFuncMod.F90 (CalcStomataResist4H2O, update_osmo_turg_pressure).

5 · Root supply, redistribution, and tissue storage

qnl,potential = [Ψc + Ψgravity − Ψsoil,l] / Rsoil+root,nl
qnl = max[−Wavailable,l fnl, min(qnl,potential, Vair,l fnl)]

Index n identifies a root/mycorrhizal category; l identifies a soil layer. Categories and rooted layers contribute in parallel. Individual pathways combine soil resistance with radial and axial root resistance; the axial routing can include a medium-root branch. The code uses total soil water potential supplied to the plant API and a gravitational adjustment for effective canopy height.

Negative q draws soil water into vegetation. Positive q permits hydraulic redistribution into soil, subject to pore space. A second check scales positive releases if canopy water, intercepted stores, and simultaneous uptake cannot support them. Root-water heat uses the donor temperature: soil temperature for uptake, vegetation temperature for release.

Qroot,out = Σq>0 cwTcq + Σq≤0 cwTsoil,lq

Water potential determines a target tissue-water store

F(Ψc) = 0.16 + 0.10|Ψc|/(0.05|Ψc| + 2)
Wtarget = 10⁻⁶ MC/F(Ψc)
DIFFZ = Wtarget − Wb,old
DIFFU = e − q
DIFFW = DIFFU − DIFFZ

get_FDM describes F as a dry-mass/water ratio, while the executed storage expression supplies the carbon variable CanopyMassC4H2OStorage. The equation above records that implementation without silently inserting a dry-mass conversion. The factor 10⁻⁶ converts grams of water to its equivalent volume under the code’s density convention.

If root supply exceeds transpiration, e − q is positive and tissue can rehydrate. Otherwise tissue water can supply some loss. The solver adjusts Ψc using estimates of storage and flow sensitivity until this supply–storage residual is small.

Sources: UptakesMod.F90 (CalcPlantHydroResistance, root loop and storage residual in CanopyEnergyH2OIter_func); PlantMathFuncMod.F90 (get_FDM).

6 · Iteration, acceptance, and state update

  1. Initialize canopy storage-associated carbon, heat capacity, hydraulic resistances, trial potential, and intercepted-precipitation heat.
  2. For each trial, update longwave emission, aerodynamic resistance, osmotic pressure/turgor, stomatal resistance, vapor exchange, latent heat, and the temperature candidate.
  3. Under-relax vegetation temperature. When its candidate mismatch is below 0.05 K, or every tenth iteration, evaluate root flows and the tissue-water residual.
  4. Use storage/flow sensitivities to adjust water potential. Relative water residual below 0.005 triggers a final stomatal update/confirmation sequence. A small potential increment after at least 30 iterations or the maximum iteration count provides alternative exits.
  5. Commit TKCanopy_pft into TKC_pft; update tissue/interception stores in UpdatePlantWaterVars; aggregate fluxes in EXTRACTs and export the plant column.
Wb,new = Wb,old + e − q
Wheld,new = Wheld,old + Prain + eliq
Wsnow,new = Wsnow,old + Psnow + esnow

The final canopy heat capacity is reconstructed from storage-associated organic matter, tissue water, intercepted liquid, and intercepted snow. HeatStorCanopy_pft is assigned T0*C0 − Tc*Cnew + HeatEvapSens + PrecpHeatbyCanopy; it should not be read as simply CΔT or as an extra ground heat transfer.

Termination is not a guarantee of a small energy residual. The code clips the temperature candidate, relaxes its update, has alternate water-potential exits, and calls HandlingDivergence at the iteration limit. That fallback modifies fluxes and states. Inactive/hydraulically inactive vegetation has a separate HandleBareSoil path.

7 · What vegetation sends to the ground

There is no single “canopy heat flux into soil” output containing all exchanges. Radiation, air feedback, root advection, and precipitation follow different variables and timing. ReceivePlantColumns copies the plant API results into global column/layer arrays.

8 · The hourly canopy-air feedback

Tqn+1 = Tan+1 − SHn / (cairA)
ρqn+1 = ρan+1 − SLn / (LvA)
SH = HgroundRground + ΣpHpRp

These are the unstabilized hourly diagnostic forms. Both temperature and vapor concentration now use resistance-based corrections when matching references and nonzero slopes are available. They do not integrate an explicit canopy-air heat store. Negative resistance-weighted sensible heat means vegetation/ground has heated air, increasing the next diagnosed Tq. Tq stays fixed while ground substeps advance within that hour.

The restored litter outer stabilizer modifies accepted sensible heat as well as radiation. Its sensible correction enters HeatSensAir2Surf_col, then the resistance-weighted store. The snow outer stabilizer directly corrects radiation, not sensible heat; its effects on air feedback arise through subsequent state and flux changes. Both retain matching local heat bookkeeping.

Implemented temperature stabilization: resistance estimate. Each active soil, litter, and snow exchange adds its step-integrated sensible conductance to SurfSensHeatConduct_col. After the full hourly heat is assembled, RedistMod::UpdateCanopyAirDerivatives applies the same above-canopy resistance weight used for surface sensible heat.
KH = Rabove Σactive ground steps,j cairAexchange,jΔtj/ra,j
Tqn+1 = [CATan+1 − SHn + KHnTqn]/[CA + KHn],   CA = cairA

The conductance sum is reset after the previous-hour predictor is consumed, then rebuilt from actual active ground substeps. This includes the proper snow/litter subcycling factors and changing aerodynamic resistance. Plant sensible heat uses ambient Ta, so its conductance is not added as a direct Tq derivative. Its complete accepted heat remains in SH.

dSensHeatdTKQ_col stores the weighted derivative; TKQHeatRef_col stores the matching completed-hour temperature. Both are saved in restarts; legacy restarts use zero derivative for the first resumed predictor and rebuild it during that hour. Zero conductance recovers the explicit predictor.

The estimate holds surface temperatures, resistances, and other state/forcing fixed when taking the derivative. It does not propagate the implicit surface-temperature response, phase changes, or indirect plant responses. It changes only the next boundary-temperature predictor and leaves completed physical heat transfers unchanged. The earlier hourly secant has been replaced; there is no ΔT threshold in the resistance calculation.

Both predictors use the explicit solution below, with x = Tq or ρq, F = S_H or S_L, C = c_air A or L_v A, and D = cfeedback × K_H or cfeedback × K_V:

x_new = [x_a − (F − D x_old)/C]/[1 + D/C]

cfeedback is a compile-time multiplier shared by both predictors: 0 recovers the explicit diagnostic, values between 0 and 1 weaken stabilization, and 1 uses the full frozen-state estimate. Stored derivatives remain unscaled. The vapor slope sums active latent-energy derivatives: L_v Cd for unconstrained soil/litter or liquid-snow exchange, L_s Cd for sublimation, and zero when the relevant donor limits are active. dLatHeatdVPQ_col and VPQLatHeatRef_col are saved together in restarts; missing fields recover the explicit first predictor.

Separate scalar predictors freeze heat–humidity cross-responses. They impose no saturation relationship between Tq and ρq. Their lag and frozen-surface slopes can affect mean conditions; numerical stability alone does not demonstrate absence of temperature or humidity bias. See the joint heat–humidity formulation.

Sources: SurfPhysMod.F90 (SetCanopyProperty); SurfLitterPhysMod.F90 (UpdateLitRBe4RunoffM); SnowPhysMod.F90 (UpdateSnowPack1M).
