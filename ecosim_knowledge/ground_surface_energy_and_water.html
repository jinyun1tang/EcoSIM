Ground-surface energy
and water dynamics

Snow, surface litter/ponded water, and the top soil layer exchange water and heat with the canopy air, with one another, and with neighboring cells. EcoSIM advances these exchanges in nested substeps, then sends ground radiation and the updated soil state to the vegetation calculation.

1 · Timing: an hour contains several coupled solves

The standalone driver calls HOUR1 → WATSUB → MicrobeModel → PlantModel → … → REDIST, with biological modules conditional on configuration. WATSUB stages the surface once and then runs NPH ground substeps. Snow has NPS inner steps; exposed litter has NPR inner steps.

Δtg = dts_HeatWatTP ≈ 1/NPH hours
Δtsnow = Δtg/NPS,   Δtlitter = Δtg/NPR

dts_HeatWatTP is initialized as 0.99999/NPH. The separate numerical transfer fraction dts_wat limits some withdrawals; it is not another physical timestep to multiply into every flux.

Sources: EcoSIMAPI.F90 (Run_EcoSIM_one_step); WatsubMod.F90 (watsub); SurfPhysMod.F90 (RunSurfacePhysModelM).

2 · Canopy fluxes: what arrives, and when?

Canopy influence reaches the ground through several distinct paths. Transpiration does not directly remove liquid from the surface litter store; root withdrawal acts at soil layers, and atmospheric feedback changes the vapor/heat boundary.

These equations show the explicit base predictor; both temperature and vapor concentration now use resistance-based corrections when available (see section 10 and the companion canopy note). 4 · Radiation, sensible heat, and vapor exchange

Incoming radiation beneath the canopy

QSW,ground = RadSWGrnd × Δtg
QLW,in,ground = (LWsky fsky→ground + LWcanopy,previous hour) Δtg
Qrad,j = (1 − αj)QSW,j + QLW,in,j − BjTj⁴
Bj = εj σ Aj Δtj

j identifies snow, litter, or exposed soil. Snow/litter inner steps receive their share of Δtg. Sky transmission uses FracSWRad2Grnd_col in the implemented longwave expression. The OTC emissivity modifier applies to canopy emission; ground snow, litter, and soil use their own emissivities.

Atmospheric sensible and latent heat

QH,j = GH,j(Tq − Tj),   GH,j ≈ cairAexchange,jΔtj/ra,j
Fv,jproposal = Gv,j(ρq − ρsurface,j),   Gv,j ≈ Aexchange,jΔtj/(ra,j + RZ)
QLE,j = LvFliquid vapor,j + LsFsnow sublimation,j

G values here are step-integrated conductances; additional pore/litter resistances enter the soil pathway. For snow and litter, the exchange area follows their surface fractions. The implemented soil conductances use A × ffree, with mixed bare/litter pathways represented by resistance; soil radiation separately uses A × ffree × fbare. Do not apply the bare fraction to the soil conductance a second time. Stability corrections change aerodynamic resistance as the temperature gradient changes. The implementation uses bounded resistance updates rather than assigning the Richardson correction without limits.

ρsurface,soil/litter = vapsat(T) exp[18 Ψ/(RGASC T)]
ρsurface,snow = vapsat(Tsnow)

Soil Ψ includes matric and osmotic potential; the litter atmospheric expression uses its calculated matric potential. These are the executed expressions in the model’s units. Vapor withdrawal is constrained by available donor liquid or dry snow; condensation can add water.

Mass enthalpy is separate from latent heat

Qatmosphere→surface = Qrad + QH + QLE + Qmass enthalpy

Evaporated liquid carries sensible enthalpy cpw × donor temperature × water transfer in addition to latent heat. Soil and snow atmospheric routines choose the surface temperature for loss and TKQ_col for incoming vapor mass. The litter inner routine uses TKR1 in its atmospheric mass-enthalpy expression. HeatAdv_scal enables/scales selected water-enthalpy paths; it is not applied identically to every routine.

Sources: SurfPhysMod.F90 (Radiation2Surface, LiterSoilRadiationM, SoilSRFEnerbyBalanceM); SurfLitterPhysMod.F90 (SurfLitterIterationM); SnowPhysMod.F90 (SnowAtmosExchangeMM).

5 · Snow: layered energy, phase change, and drainage

AtmLandSurfExchangeM first calls SolveSnowpackM when top-layer heat capacity is significant. Snowfall, rain on snow, and ice inputs are divided among its NPS inner steps. Atmosphere–snow exchange is followed by internal transfer, drainage, and source-limited freezing/melting.

ΔWsnow,total = snowfallSWE + rain + ice inputSWE + atmospheric gain − drainage + net redistribution
Enew = Eold + Qatmosphere + Qprecip + Qinternal,in−out + Qphase

Freezing releases heat (Qphase > 0); melting consumes it. Phase changes redistribute solid and liquid storage but do not change total water-equivalent mass. The phase calculation uses material remaining after accepted transfers and the code’s Kirchhoff/relaxation factors; the expression above is an accounting identity, not a replacement for those kinetics.

Atmospheric water loss uses liquid evaporation first, then dry-snow sublimation. The bottom snow layer can send water and associated heat to litter or directly to soil micropores/macropores. The resulting transfers initialize WatFLo2LitrM_col, HeatFLoByWat2LitRM_col, WaterFlow2Micpt_3D, WaterFlow2Macpt_3D, and HeatFlow2Soili_3D before exposed-surface contributions are added.

UpdateSnowPack1M reconstructs the outer-step snow state before redistribution. The top-layer longwave stabilizer is retained, with its correction returned to net snow radiation and included in snow heat accumulation and outgoing longwave. Snow redistribution is proposed from the common post-local state and donor-limited before application.

Detailed companion: EcoSIM snow dynamics. Source: SnowPhysMod.F90.

6 · Exposed litter: a water and heat reservoir

On snow-free area, the surface calculation evaluates exposed-soil atmospheric exchange and then the litter–soil interaction. SurfLitterIterationM advances a litter temperature predictor over NPR inner steps, receiving rain and accepted snow inputs and exchanging heat/vapor with topsoil.

ΔWlitter = Plitter + Fair→litter − Flitter→soil,vapor
Qlitter→soil = Qconduction + Qvapor enthalpy
Elitter,new = Elitter,old + Qatmosphere + Qrain − Qlitter→soil + Qimplicit correction

Conductive heat is bounded by a litter–soil equilibration estimate so that one transfer does not exceed its allowed approach to a shared temperature. Internal vapor transfer carries latent heat and donor sensible enthalpy, with equal and opposite contributions to litter and soil. Atmospheric litter evaporation has first claim on liquid before a litter-to-soil vapor withdrawal is accepted.

After these inner steps, SurfLitrSoilWaterExchangeM handles liquid infiltration; UpdateLitterPhaseChangeM computes freezing/thawing; UpdateLitRBe4RunoffM applies the accepted net water/heat and reconstructs litter temperature. It retains the historical outer longwave/sensible stabilizer in addition to the inner implicit solve.

Qphase,litter > 0 ⇒ freezing;   Vthaw,SWE = −Qphase,litter/Lfusion
Vliquid,new = Vliquid,old + ΔWexternal + Vthaw,SWE
Vice,new = Vice,old − Vthaw,SWE/DENSICE

The freezing temperature is calculated from litter water potential by get_Tfrez. Freezing and melting heat are limited by available liquid/ice; the additional dts_wat factors govern numerical transfer rates.

7 · Infiltration shares one available-water budget

SurfLitrSoilWaterExchangeM constructs available litter/soil water and receiving pore space after atmospheric and snow fluxes have already been proposed. It handles micropores first, then gives macropores only the liquid still available.

Vlitter,avail = max(0, Vlitter,start + ΔWaccepted local)
gls = 2KlKs / (KlΔzs + KsΔzl)
Fls,potential = gls(Ψtotal,l − Ψtotal,s) A feffective litter Δtg
Ψtotal = Ψmatric + Ψgravity + Ψosmotic

The hydraulic conductivities use moisture classes; soil conductivity is multiplied by RainEkReducedKsat. In standalone soil cells this is a Darcy-type proposal. Saturation corrections and receiving pore space further constrain it, followed by a shared donor-budget bound. Negative accepted flow permits soil-to-litter return. ATS coupling suppresses this local Darcy conductance; water-surface cells use a mobile-water transfer branch.

−Vsoil,avail ≤ Fmic,accepted ≤ Vlitter,avail
Vremain = max(0, Vlitter,avail − Fmic,accepted)
Fmac = min(Vmobile,remainingdts_wat, Vremain, Vmacropore space)

Mobile liquid is computed from the liquid fraction of water+ice above litter holding capacity. After each accepted liquid transfer, the same water and donor-temperature heat are credited to the soil face and debited from litter:

Qadv = cwTdonorFaccepted × HeatAdv_scal

WatFLoLitr2SoilM_col and WatFLoLitr2MacPM_col retain accepted transfers for downstream material transport. Solute transport must use these accepted water pathways, not independently recomputed infiltration proposals.

The litter-to-soil liquid heat uses the donor temperature stored at this stage of the split update. Local water conservation and paired heat transfer do not, by themselves, establish that all fluxes used a common fully updated donor temperature.

Source: SurfPhysMod.F90, SurfLitrSoilWaterExchangeM. Canopy interception also affects infiltration indirectly: direct rain versus canopy throughfall changes kinetic-energy impact and RainEkReducedKsat.

8 · Topsoil temperature is accepted after surface exchange

SoilSRFEnerbyBalanceM supplies an atmospheric heat predictor. Its vapor flux is limited by top-layer liquid; incoming water is added to the working budget before subsequent litter exchange. WATSUB then combines surface-face heat with subsurface conduction/advection, irrigation, phase change, and staged root-water heat.

CnewTnew = Eold + Qbase + Qphase + Qroot + J(Tnew − Told)
Tnew = [Eold + Qbase + Qphase + Qroot − JTold] / (Cnew − J)
J = −4BsoilTold³ − GH,soil ≤ 0

This expresses the topsoil atmospheric derivative in UpdateSoilMoistTempM; water and root transfers can change heat capacity and require additional bookkeeping in the executed branches. J is dHGrnddTKsoi1, a derivative of step-integrated heat, in MJ K⁻¹, not a heat rate.

CorrectSoilSurfaceHeatM records the accepted correction JΔT in surface-face heat, cell/hourly heat totals, sensible heat, net radiation, and outgoing longwave. Its caller adds the same correction to THeatFlow2Soil_3DM_vr. The exposed-soil gate is matched when forming J; a snow-covered step starts with zero exposed-soil sensible derivative.

Prescribed soil warming is applied separately when enabled. Its heat increment is recorded in HeatSource_vr; it is an imposed source rather than canopy exchange. Deeper profile flow and water-table/tile drainage are outside this surface-focused walkthrough: see water-table-modulated exchange.

Sources: WatsubMod.F90 (UpdateSoilMoistTempM); SurfPhysMod.F90 (CorrectSoilSurfaceHeatM).

9 · Surface runoff: propose, limit, then apply

Runoff proposals are constructed only after every cell has completed its local surface processes. This common post-local state avoids reading a mixture of updated and unupdated neighbors. The potential is based on mobile surface water above retention capacity and a Manning-style speed:

Vexcess = max(0, XVLMobileWaterLitR − VWatStoreCapSurf)
h = Vexcess/A,   Rhydraulic = h/2.828
vcode = Rhydraulic0.67 √S / SoiSurfRoughness

The implementation converts this into its grid-scaled runoff proposal, caps it by excess storage and liquid, and multiplies by the mobile liquid fraction. Face construction uses neighboring elevations/water levels, directional slope weights, and boundary configuration. These expressions describe the implemented parameterization, rather than a general channel-discharge formula.

Oi = Σoutgoing faces f Fifproposal
λi = min[1, max(0,Vliquid,i − tiny_water)/Oi],   Oi > 0
Fifaccepted = λiFifproposal,   Qifaccepted = λiQifproposal

LimitSurfaceRunoffM limits the sum of all withdrawals from each donor. Concurrent inflows are not credited to that donor budget. The same accepted shared faces determine donor losses, receiver gains, transport fluxes, and hourly water/heat corrections. UpdateSurfaceAtM applies the net runoff and snow-redistribution updates.

The donor limiter prevents runoff from over-drawing available liquid and preserves paired face transfers. It does not prevent several high cells from collectively overfilling a low cell or guarantee monotonic water-level equilibration. Adaptive substeps remain relevant for convergent flow into depressions.

Sources: SurfPhysMod.F90 (CalcSurfaceRunoffPotentialM, AggregateSurfRunoffFluxM, LimitSurfaceRunoffM); SurfLitterPhysMod.F90 (XGridsSurfRunoffM, UpdateLitRAftRunoffM). Detailed companion: surface runoff calculation.

10 · Stabilizers must carry their implied heat correction

A common algebraic form captures the restored outer litter/snow updates and the soil atmospheric derivative. Let D ≥ 0 be an effective step heat sensitivity:

Tnew = [Eold + Qbase + Qphase + DTold] / (Cnew + D)
ΔQstabilizer = −D(Tnew − Told)
Cnew(Tnew − Told) = [Qbase + Qphase − (Cnew − Cold)Told] Cnew/(Cnew + D)

The temperature increment is damped relative to the unstabilized reconstruction. Recording ΔQ makes its resulting stored heat agree with the accepted update; applying only the denominator correction would change heat storage without the matching transfer.

Outer litter/snow damping is additional numerical stabilization on top of their inner solves. Local algebraic heat consistency does not prove timestep independence or physical accuracy. The amplified litter coefficient should not be interpreted as an additional physical radiation Jacobian.

Surface stabilization does not directly damp the diagnosed hourly TKQ_col. Its sensible-heat corrections can change the next hour’s resistance-weighted air feedback. The next-hour temperature predictor now uses KH estimated from the hourly sum of active soil/litter/snow sensible conductances, multiplied by the same resistance weight as the accepted surface heat. Surface temperatures and resistances are held fixed in this derivative; plant heat remains in the total forcing. The vapor predictor also uses the active, donor-limited latent-energy derivative, including the sublimation latent heat for snow. Both slopes are scaled locally by the compile-time cfeedback multiplier (0: explicit, 1: full estimate); stored slopes remain unscaled. UpdateCanopyAirDerivatives saves both slopes and reference states. There is no finite-difference ΔT threshold, no imposed canopy-air saturation bound, and completed physical heat transfers are unchanged. See the canopy-air feedback discussion.

For a combined snow+litter+soil budget, internal transfers cancel when their two sides use the same accepted water/heat. External terms are precipitation, atmospheric exchange, boundary runoff/runon, imposed sources, and exchanges across the selected soil-domain boundary. Root exchange cancels only if the plant reservoir is included in that combined budget.

Current implementation caveat: the litter wrapper SurfLitREnergyBalanceM adds radiation, sensible heat, and vapor mass enthalpy to HeatNet2Surf_col, but omits its separately recorded LatentHeatAir2LitR. The litter temperature’s accepted atmospheric heat includes that latent term. Consequently, a successful local stabilizer check does not establish that the aggregate surface heat ledger includes every contribution. This description does not silently treat that existing omission as corrected.

Useful diagnostic checks are: paired infiltration/runoff heat and water; snow water-equivalent totals; accepted stored heat versus net heat plus phase heat; and component-by-component closure of the surface ledger. Finite output or a completed smoke run alone establishes neither conservation nor scientific accuracy.
