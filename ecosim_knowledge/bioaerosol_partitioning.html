Partitioning deposited bioaerosols in EcoSIM

Modeling knowledge note · 18 September 2026 · Proposed application to Biosphere 2 LEO

Partition deposited material by its biological and chemical identity first, then separate microbial material into viable and dead fractions. A viable/dead split alone does not describe plant debris, mineral dust, or dissolved inputs.

Status: The six input categories and their routing are present in the current reader and deposition source. This note distinguishes that implementation from proposed LEO fractions and chemistry; it does not establish a measured LEO partition or validated simulation behavior. The numerical fractions and stoichiometry below are proposed sensitivity-study assumptions, not measured LEO values. Absolute deposition magnitude remains an independent input. Associated mineral dust and dissolved inputs are included for deposition bookkeeping; they are not all bioaerosols in the strict biological-particle sense.

Partitioning pathway

Total deposited material
├─ Microbial material
│  ├─ Viable cells/spores → microbial functional groups
│  │                        (active or dormant; establishment required)
│  └─ Dead cells/fragments → microbial residue pools
├─ Non-microbial biological material
│  ├─ Viable propagules → appropriate establishment pathway
│  └─ Dead tissue/pollen/fragments → litter pools
├─ Dissolved organic material → DOC / DON / DOP
└─ Associated inorganic material
   ├─ Available nutrients → inorganic nutrient pools
   └─ Mineral particles → mineral/soil inputs

Use mutually exclusive categories at the receiving boundary. For example, material already dissolved in deposition water should not also be counted as particulate dead biomass.

Quantitative baseline for LEO

Normalize to 100 units of deposited organic carbon, including the organic material assigned to DOM at the receiving boundary. These percentages are carbon-mass fractions, not particle counts, viability counts, or percentages of total aerosol dry mass. Inorganic nutrients and mineral dust have separate fluxes and are outside this denominator.

The identity-first hierarchy is therefore 70% microbial material, 10% other biological material, and 20% DOM. Within microbial C, 14.29% is viable and 85.71% dead. Within other biological C, 80% is dead detritus, 10% viable moss, and 10% viable lichen. “Non-microbial” is an operational EcoSIM label: intact lichens contain microbial partners but enter the nonvascular plant pathway. Do not count those partners again as free microbial deposition.

This baseline deliberately emphasizes dead organic substrate while retaining a smaller living inoculum. The 1% moss and 1% lichen shares are exploratory establishment inputs; their equality is not evidence of equal atmospheric abundance. Deposition does not guarantee establishment. Set both to zero in a no-propagule control and transfer their combined 2% C to dead biological detritus. Woody debris is zero in this baseline.

The microbial benchmark comes from Cleveland and Liptzin (2007), a synthesis of soil microbial biomass, not airborne necromass. Its use assumes recently dead cells with little selective nutrient loss. The DOM choice is a modeling prior consistent with the broad atmospheric DOM variability reported by Galletti et al. (2020); that marine-influenced deposition study does not establish a LEO-specific ratio or any of the carbon fractions above.

As an initial residue allocation, put 70% of dead microbial C/N/P into component 1 (kinetic) and 30% into component 2 (recalcitrant). This gives 42% and 18% of total deposited organic C, respectively. Using the same split for each element preserves bulk stoichiometry; component-specific nutrient enrichment would require an additional constrained allocation. The 70:30 split is a scenario assumption.

These N and P values cover DOM, dead microbes, and dead non-microbial fragments under the recommended chemistry. Add the element fluxes associated with the remaining biological fractions using their own chemistry to obtain the full budget. DOM here is already separated from particles at the receiving boundary; if generated by subsequent cell lysis, transfer it out of the parent pool rather than adding it again.

Scope: Current routing and coefficients are described from source inspection, not a build or simulation test. If several PFTs match the same moss or lichen prefix, the routine assigns the full category flux to each; a category-level budget therefore needs a within-category allocation. N/P accounting and downstream establishment also require verification before claiming full elemental closure. No Fortran or input dataset is changed by this documentation update.

Why fine litter for fresh dead microbial deposition?

Complex 2 provides an existing surface-active receiving complex. Within it, the microbial-residue pool offers kinetics designed for microbial remains. Assigning dead microbes there avoids forcing all microbial material into plant-litter chemistry.

OMBioResdu has two residue components, named kinetic and recalcitrant; these are distinct from the four SolidOM components. EcoSIM hydrolyzes residues with SPORC(M) and transfers their C/N/P to dissolved organic pools. Sources: MicBGCPars.F90, lines 184–188; MicBGCFGMod.F90, around lines 1433–1451 and 1642–1647.

POM (K = 4) and humus (K = 5) are excluded from surface litter-layer calculations. They are therefore not default destinations for fresh surface deposition. Resuspended soil particles can carry previously processed organic matter resembling these pools, but that case needs an explicit receiving-layer and transformation treatment. See complexes and layer coverage and solid organic matter kinetics.

The receiving pools are now represented in the deposition source. Simulation validation must still verify layer availability, state/API mapping, units, and balance diagnostics. In particular, adding dead substrate does not seed living decomposers: residue hydrolysis depends on microbial activity, so a model starting with zero viable microbes may not decompose the added material.

Mass and elemental accounting

State whether the supplied flux is total particle mass, biological dry mass, or carbon mass. They are not interchangeable. For each element E (C, N, or P), conserve deposition across the selected destinations:

F_E,total = F_E,viable microbes + F_E,dead microbes
          + F_E,other biological material + F_E,dissolved organic
          + F_E,inorganic

This is an elemental budget, not a sum of all dry particle mass: mineral matter can contain little organic carbon. Include relevant inorganic carbon separately if present.

Within microbial material, define F_E,viable = f_v,E × F_E,microbial and F_E,dead = (1 − f_v,E) × F_E,microbial. Applying one viable fraction to C, N, and P assumes identical elemental composition in viable and dead material. A viability estimate based on cell counts also needs biomass weighting before it can be used as a carbon fraction.

For a flux in g E m⁻² h⁻¹, multiply by receiving area and timestep in hours only when the destination stores total elemental mass. If it stores mass per area, omit the area multiplier. Record deposited C/N/P as external inputs and avoid double counting nutrients already included in an existing deposition forcing.

What is established for LEO?

Sengupta et al. (2019) inferred air as a likely source of some archaea in miniLEO because they were absent from sampled parent material and irrigation water. This supports a possible airborne immigration pathway; it does not quantify viable/dead deposition, organic-matter composition, or deposition flux. Assessing Microbial Community Patterns During Incipient Soil Formation From Basalt.

The broader biological-aerosol literature describes microbes, spores, pollen, and biological fragments, supporting the need to distinguish material categories. It does not supply a LEO-specific partition. Després et al. (2012), Primary biological aerosol particles in the atmosphere: a review.

For an initial LEO sensitivity study, vary deposition magnitude, viable microbial fraction, microbial versus plant-derived organic fraction, residue resistance partition, and C:N:P composition. Label these as scenario assumptions until constrained by measurements.
