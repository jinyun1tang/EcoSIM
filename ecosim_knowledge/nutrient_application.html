EcoSIM process walkthrough

Nutrient application

Management supplies elemental C, N, P, and Ca to specified cells and dates. EcoSIM converts those inputs into fertilizer reservoirs, dissolved nutrients, precipitated minerals, and organic pools. Nutrient application, subsequent release, and plant uptake are distinct steps.

ReadManagementFiles chooses the fertilizer table for each topographic unit and applies its records over NH1:NH2, NV1:NV2. The unit count does not limit its cell coordinates: a unit can cover several cells. Amounts and application settings are stored by day and cell. The annual forcing setup clears and rereads these schedules.

Hour1Mod calls ApplyFertilizerAtNoon during hourly preparation. For each cell, application occurs when J == INT(SolarNoonHour_col(NY,NX)). The routine calls ApplyMineralFertilizer, ApplyManure, and ApplyUreaNitrifierInhibitor, in that order. There is no application-hour field in the fertilizer record.

Application is not immediate availability. The mineral N pools are subsequently depleted by dissolution/release and urea hydrolysis in SoluteMod. Precipitated P minerals participate in geochemical reactions; organic inputs require biological processing to release mineral nutrients.

Sources: management reader, hourly caller, application routines, subsequent release.

3 · Choose the receiving layer before adding mass

Let d be FDPTH(day,NY,NX), and let zb be CumDepz2LayBottom_vr(NU_col−1,NY,NX). The absolute placement coordinate is d* = d + zb.

Mineral fertilizer

If d* ≤ 0 and the application has no banded material, the code selects surface layer 0. It partitions most broadcast material between litter and topsoil:

flitter = 1 − exp[−0.008 (Clitter/A₀)]
ftopsoil = 1 − flitter.

Clitter is SoilOrgM_vr(ielmc,0,NY,NX) [g C per cell] and A₀ is the layer-zero area [m²]. The coefficient follows the implemented g C m⁻² basis. At zero litter C, this fraction is zero.

Otherwise, the code chooses the first eligible soil layer whose bottom reaches d*. The full application enters that layer. A depth below the profile produces an explicit error.

Organic amendments

ApplyManure recomputes placement from the same prescribed depth. If d* ≤ 0, it selects layer 0; otherwise it searches active layers NU_col:NL_col and rejects a depth below the profile.

There is no exponential litter/topsoil split for organic material: the selected layer receives the amendment’s organic pools. Recomputing organic placement does not change the mineral destinations already recorded for inhibitor treatment.

The organic block runs when the combined plant-residue and manure C input is positive.

Two material-specific exceptions: surface NH₃ fertilizer bypasses litter and enters topsoil. Limestone and gypsum are added to the current topsoil precipitate pools, even when a different mineral application layer was diagnosed.

Source: ApplyMineralFertilizer and ApplyManure. Layer 0 represents surface litter; NU_col identifies current topsoil.

For instance, 3.1 g P m⁻² over 10 m² supplies 31 g P: 1 mol of elemental P, 0.5 mol of monocalcium-phosphate formula units, or ⅓ mol of apatite formula units. These choices preserve the same P input but do not imply the same solubility or availability.

The N fertilizer reservoirs release material later. For broadcast NH₄ in a soil layer, the source contains terms of the form:

RNH₄→unbanded = kNH₄ FNH₄ funbanded θwater
RNH₄→band = kNH₄ FNH₄ fband θwater.

These are the terms as assembled in the geochemical update; its solver controls time integration and coupling to solution pools. NH₃ release and urea hydrolysis have their own equations. Application alone therefore does not place all N immediately into the dissolved NH₄/NO₃ pools.

6 · Plant residues and animal manure carry joint C/N/P budgets

ApplyManure handles two separate input slots: plant residues go to the fine-litter organic complex (k_fine_comp), and animal manure goes to the manure complex (k_manure). The residue/manure type selects the composition weights for protein, soluble carbohydrate, cellulose, and lignin, with associated N/P weighting parameters.

Element-specific weights

Microbial allocations are limited by the remaining C, N, and P donor budgets. The shared reservation factor includes represented autotroph shares so those additions cannot each consume an unrestricted copy of the same donor mass. Allocations within a functional group are divided among its represented guilds.

After biomass allocation, the DOC/DON/DOP increments are separately limited to the remaining donor amounts:

DE = max[0, min(0.1 BE, E₀ − BE)]   for E ∈ {C,N,P}
Eremaining = max(0, E₀ − BE − DE).

BE denotes cumulative microbial allocation of element E. Remaining carbon is distributed by CFOSC; remaining N and P use separately normalized component weights involving CNOFC and CPOFC. This lets nutrient-rich residue retain N/P allocations even when little carbon remains after the earlier steps, provided the corresponding weights are nonzero.

Organic N/P are not immediately mineral fertilizer. These inputs enter microbial biomass, DOM, and solid organic pools. Subsequent decomposition, mineralization, immobilization, and transport govern when they become available to plants.

The organic branch requires positive combined residue/manure C. Specify organic C, N, and P together; do not use an organic slot as a substitute for a carbon-free mineral nutrient input.

Source: ApplyManure; composition identifiers in ElmIDMod.

7 · Banding creates spatial domains within a layer

Band and non-band pools represent different chemical environments. They are not two interchangeable names for the same pool. New banded applications initialize or update band geometry for NH₄-related, nitrate-related, and phosphate-related domains.

fband,L = min[0.999, (wband,L/srow) (hband,L/ΔzL)]
fnonband,L = 1 − fband,L.

The application code diagnoses widths and thicknesses using the prescribed placement and row spacing. When a band geometry changes, associated existing dissolved, exchangeable, and precipitated pools are redistributed using the new domain fractions. That redistribution is separate from adding the new fertilizer input.

Broadcast explicit phosphate and broadcast phosphate minerals are partitioned across the current phosphate-domain fractions. The explicitly banded addition enters the band pool. For soluble P in one receiving layer, after applying its placement fraction f:

ΔPnonband = f mP,broadcast fnonband
ΔPband = f (mP,broadcast fband + mP,banded)
ΔPnonband + ΔPband = f (mP,broadcast + mP,banded).

Do not read a banded pool as additional fertilizer on top of the total prescribed amount. Domain fractions partition broadcast material, while explicitly banded material is a separately prescribed portion.

Inhibitors follow the actual mineral destination

IR0 controls the urea-hydrolysis treatment and, for values 3 or 4, nitrification inhibition. The application routine records receiving-layer masks before organic placement can change LFDPTH. Inhibitor activity is initialized as an intensive activity of 1 in selected layers, rather than as a mass split by the litter fraction. The model then evolves those activities in its reaction calculations.

Source: band geometry, pool updates, and ApplyUreaNitrifierInhibitor.

9 · Input accounting and provenance

QuantityApplication accounting
Mineral NTZIN and FertN_Flx_CumYr_col receive the elemental N mass represented by all mineral N additions.
Mineral PTPIN and FerP_Flx_CumYr_col recover explicit P plus 2 × P atomic mass per monocalcium-phosphate unit and 3 × P atomic mass per apatite unit.
Organic C/N/PtAmendOrgC_lnd, TORGN, and TORGP record the corresponding organic inputs. Annual amendment-C and fertilizer-N/P accumulators also receive their respective organic additions.
External versus internally returned CPlant residue contributes its full added C to application-day NBP. Manure uses ExternalManureC_col, captured when inputs are read, so grazing returns merged into manure do not become a new external C input.

These ledgers overlap in purpose: a total-input ledger and an annual summary can record the same input. Do not add them together as independent nutrient sources. Similarly, transfers from fertilizer reservoirs to solution or from organic matter to mineral nutrients are internal redistributions after the external input has already been booked.

A useful elemental check is ΔEfertilizer reservoirs + ΔEsolution + ΔEminerals + ΔEorganic pools = Eexternal input for an isolated application, with internally returned material and other simultaneous fluxes accounted for separately. Pool-specific units must be converted to elemental mass before summing.
