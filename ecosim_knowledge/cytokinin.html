Cytokinin in EcoSIM

A transported root signal connects local growth metabolism to the balance between root elongation and thickening.

1. What is represented?

EcoSIM carries three cytokinin concentrations through its root system. Fine roots and mycorrhizae produce the signal in proportion to growth respiration. Water uptake carries it into medium and coarse roots; medium roots also produce a smaller local source. In coarse roots, upward advection and diffusion redistribute it among soil layers. The concentrations influence the allocation of growth toward thicker roots.

Scope of the root representation. Medium roots are optional and are represented only for trees. Trees without medium roots use the direct fine-root-to-coarse-root pathway. Cytokinin dynamics for non-tree PFTs are for diagnostic purposes only; the growth-regulating role described here applies to the tree pathway.

Current implementation: CytoKininDynamics runs for the model’s woody vascular pathway: iPlantRootProfile_pft > 1 and iPlant2ndGrothPattern_pft == 1. It returns when there are no structural root axes. Thus this transport calculation is not applied universally to every PFT.

A calibrated signal scale. Source comments state that production is amplified by 10³, and the response constants use the same scaled convention. Interpret numerical concentrations together with that calibration. The implementation does not distinguish cytokinin chemical species or include an explicit shoot cytokinin pool in this pathway.

Sources: RootMod.F90, CytoKininDynamics; PlantMathFuncMod.F90, is_plant_woody_vascular.

2. The pools, their volumes, and population scaling

Fine roots / mycorrhizaeCytokinin2ndConc_rpvr(N,L,NR,NZ)

Separate concentrations for plant roots (N=1) and mycorrhizae (N=2).

Medium roots · optionalCytokininMRConc_rpvr(L,NR,NZ)

Tree-only compartment, with one concentration per soil layer and structural-axis group when present.

Coarse rootsCytokinin1stConc_rpvr(L,NR,NZ)

One concentration per layer and structural-axis group; transported vertically.

L is the soil layer, NR the structural-axis index, and NZ the PFT. The API operates on a column; the persistent arrays also carry grid coordinates. The 1st and 2nd names refer to primary and secondary roots in this representation.

Stored concentration: C   ·   Associated inventory: M = C V

The volume must have the same population and spatial basis as the source and flow. Root counts and total lengths already contain population scaling; multiplying by population again would double-count it.

Medium-root volume therefore follows root length, not soil-layer thickness. Its aggregate area is summed across axis groups without multiplying the root count a second time. Coarse-root area combines population-scaled geometry, a radius-dependent lumen fraction, and, for mature roots, a canopy-supported sapwood-area limit.

Units: C is expressed on the model’s cytokinin mass-per-water-volume scale. API comments use gC m⁻³ H₂O, while history labels use 10⁻³ gC m⁻³. Population lengths and counts have the model’s spatial normalization (d-2 in several comments); inventories, volumes, and flow must share that basis. These inconsistent labels should not be interpreted as automatic unit conversions.

3. What happens in one hour?

  1. Use the existing signal to allocate growth. SummarizeRootSink uses coarse-root concentration to calculate growth sink strengths.
  2. Grow roots and calculate production. Root biochemistry updates geometry and growth-linked cytokinin sources. Medium-root growth uses the existing medium-root concentration to divide growth between elongation and thickening.
  3. Build the transport geometry. CytoKininDynamics reconstructs conducting areas and volumes, partitions water uptake among axes, and accumulates upward coarse-root flow.
  4. Apply local sources, decay, and lateral transfers. Fine-root production and decay precede fine-root export. Medium roots receive their share, add local production, decay, then export. Existing coarse-root cytokinin decays before it receives this hour’s lateral inputs.
  5. Redistribute the coarse-root signal. Upward advection is followed by vertical diffusion. Updated concentrations become available for the next growth update.

This is an explicitly ordered hourly feedback. Growth and transport are not iterated together to convergence. Despite the routine’s “steady-state model” comment, it retains concentrations between calls and advances them using one-hour production, decay, and transport steps.

Source: RootMod.F90, calls near lines 102–115 and 286; CytoKininDynamics near 4717–5010.

4. Production and decay

Production follows RGrowCO2_Oltd, the growth-respiration rate after the implemented oxygen, water-related, and nutrient constraints. Nutrient uptake influences this signal through growth metabolism rather than entering as a direct cytokinin source.

Pf,N = Rgrowth,N RCytoK(N)
Pm = 0.1 Rgrowth,m RCytoK(1)
CompartmentProduction coefficientDecay constant, h⁻¹

These are initialized constants in PlantBGCPars. The fine-root update, written in mass form for positive volume and nonnegative input state, is:

Mf* = (Cf Vf + Pf Δt) exp(−kf Δt),   Δt = 1 h

The code actually adds P/V to concentration and then applies exponential decay and a nonnegative bound. Thus newly produced cytokinin receives the full step’s decay. This is a split source/decay update, not the exact continuously forced decay solution. Medium-root production and decay can continue when water flow is zero; its production-rate array is reset before each root-growth pass.

Sources: RootMod.F90, Grow2ndRootAxes near line 683, GrowMediumRootAxes near 1155; PlantBGCPars.F90, lines 261–263.

5. Conservative transfers between root classes

Water flow and routing

For an absorbing root type, the layer uptake is distributed among structural-axis groups in proportion to their fine-root counts:

QN,L,NR = −RPlantRootH2OUptkN,L × nN,L,NR / nN,L,total

This applies only when uptake is negative in the model’s sign convention and the total count is positive; otherwise Q is zero. Reverse flow is not represented in this cytokinin transfer path.

The fraction passing through medium roots is the geometric approximation:

f = ℓm,total / (ℓm,total + Δz × PlantPopuLive)

If medium-root volume is absent, f is zero and export goes directly to coarse roots. The fraction is calculated independently of water flow. For each fine-root/mycorrhizal donor, the two recipient shares use one exported mass.

Fine roots → medium and coarse roots

Mfnew = Mf* Vf / (Vf + Q Δt)
Ef = Mf* − Mfnew
Medium credit = f Ef   ·   Direct coarse credit = (1 − f) Ef

This is a backward-Euler, well-mixed outflow update. It debits the fine-root pool exactly once, keeps it nonnegative, and limits export to its available inventory. Zero flow causes no transfer. A zero-volume donor exports nothing; the transfer loop also requires a positive coarse receiving volume.

Medium roots → coarse roots

Let Im = ΣN f Ef,N and Qm = ΣN f QN. The medium-root step is:

Mm* = max(0, Cm Vm + Im + Pm Δt) exp(−kf,1 Δt)
Mmnew = Mm* Vm / (Vm + Qm Δt)
Em = Mm* − Mmnew   ·   Coarse credit = Em

Recipient mass is converted back to concentration using its own volume. The local coarse concentration before vertical transport is:

Cc* = Cc exp(−kc Δt) + [ΣN(1 − f) Ef,N + Em] / Vc

New lateral inputs are not subjected to coarse-root decay until a later call. Medium-bound fine-root input, however, passes through the medium decay step immediately. That difference follows the actual update order.

6. Vertical transport in coarse roots

Advection toward the upper root boundary

Coarse-root water flow is accumulated upward from deeper layers and divided by the population conducting area to obtain sap velocity, u = Qc/Ac. Despite its name, Root1stVH2O_rpvr is used here as accumulated water flow, not the storage volume Vc.

advect_remap_mass_loss reverses the soil-layer ordering into an upward coordinate. It displaces cell edges, carries cell mass on that moving grid, and remaps cumulative mass onto the fixed grid. If necessary it subdivides the step to prevent problematic edge crossing inside the domain.

The bottom edge has zero prescribed velocity. Cytokinin can leave through the upper root boundary. Boundary loss is handled once by the remap; CytoKininDynamics does not request the optional lost_mass output or credit that export to a shoot pool. The transport routine uses an internal 10⁶ mass scaling, then reverses it for concentration; its optional loss value retains that internal scale.

Diffusion along each coarse-root axis

Deff(T) = [4.3 × 10−5 × 1.03(T − 298.15) / 24] / 1.2

This is the implemented diffusivity, used in m² h⁻¹, with T in kelvin and a fixed tortuosity divisor of 1.2. At 298.15 K it is about 1.49 × 10⁻⁶ m² h⁻¹.

solve_root_diffusion_step uses implicit diffusion with storage AΔz and zero flux at both ends. Each layer interface has one shared conductance, constructed with harmonic means of area and D/Δz. The same interfacial exchange enters the two adjacent balances with opposite signs, conserving the volume-weighted inventory for valid positive geometry. Diffusion is within each structural axis; the routine does not mix cytokinin between separate axes.

Sources: RootMod.F90, coarse transport near lines 4965–5000; PlantMathFuncMod.F90, advect_remap_mass_loss near line 358 and solve_root_diffusion_step near 587.

7. How the signal changes root growth

This growth feedback describes the tree pathway. Cytokinin dynamics in non-tree PFTs serve a diagnostic purpose.

The common response function is:

F(C) = min[1, h C / {1.2 + C(1 + C/12)}],   h = enh_cyto_pft

The sensitivity h is read from the PFT parameter CYTOS. At zero concentration the response is zero. It increases at low concentration, then declines at sufficiently high concentration because of the C² term. Before the upper cap, the peak occurs at C = √(1.2 × 12) ≈ 3.79 in the internal concentration scale. A larger concentration therefore does not always mean stronger thickening.

Coarse roots: alter sink strength

For eligible coarse-root layers, SummarizeRootSink sets primary-root sink strength proportional to PrimaryRootCd4NonstTP × F(Cc), and multiplies the fine-root sink expression by 1 − F(Cc). These are growth-allocation sink weights, not direct additions of biomass.

Eligibility additionally requires the coarse-root switch, sufficient root age, and the implemented root-tip/depth criteria. Where those conditions fail, the cytokinin scalar for coarse-root thickening is set to zero.

Medium roots: divide elongation and thickening

fthick = F(Cm) max(0, 1 − Rm/dmax)
felong = 1 − fthick,   dmax = 0.015 m

When net medium-root growth is positive, the elongation share determines added root length. Total structural biomass is then updated, and radius is inferred from biomass and length. The response therefore influences root shape as well as the later conducting volume and transport rate.

Sources: RootMod.F90, func_cyto near line 5217, GrowMediumRootAxes near 1135, SummarizeRootSink near 5457–5517; PlantInfoMod.F90, CYTOS loading.

8. Persistent state and diagnostic output

The three concentration arrays are allocated and initially zeroed in RootDataType, transferred between column APIs and persistent grid state by PlantRootAxisTransferMod, and supported by restart read/write handling in RestartMod.

History fieldInterpretation
Cytokinin1stConc_pvrArithmetic mean of coarse-root concentrations across structural axes in each layer. It is not a lumen-volume-weighted concentration or a total mass.
Cytok_scalar_pvrArithmetic mean of the coarse-root growth-response scalar across structural axes.

Both fields are registered with time averaging and are inactive by default. The concentration output is labeled 1.e-3gC/m3; its update averages the internal values without an explicit 10⁻³ conversion. Compare concentrations and response constants on a consistent internal scale before interpreting measured hormone concentrations.

Sources: RootDataType.F90; PlantRootAxisTransferMod.F90; RestartMod.F90, near lines 4551–4595; HistUpdatePlant.F90, lines 429–444; HistRegisterSoilPhysRoot.F90, lines 341–352.

9. What conservation does—and does not—mean here

The corrected local transfers conserve cytokinin mass between their donor and recipient pools. With fixed volumes and with production, decay, and boundary export disabled, their sum is unchanged. In the full update, the intended accounting is:

Change in root inventory = production − decay − upper-boundary export

This identity needs qualifications when diagnosing the whole model:

  • Geometry evolves. Concentration is persistent while volumes are reconstructed. This routine does not generally rescale an old concentration by an old/new volume ratio to conserve inventory during growth or root loss.
  • Zero-volume handling clears concentrations. This is an explicit numerical treatment, not a modeled redistribution of residual hormone.
  • Upper-boundary loss is not recorded by this caller. The root profile is an open system under upward advection; there is no corresponding shoot-pool credit in this pathway.
  • Cytokinin is a growth-linked signal. Its production is derived from respiration; the inspected source assignments do not introduce a separate stoichiometric cytokinin C/N/P pool and matching substrate withdrawal.
  • Update ordering matters. Source rates must represent the current growth pass. Skipped growth branches and changing geometry need consideration in a full lifecycle budget; conservative transport alone does not establish that budget.

Validation of the current transfer changes included 109 focused geometry and transport checks plus 16 ten-day smoke cases, excluding dryland2. The focused checks isolate local transfers from later vertical transport. Smoke completion and finite outputs establish execution health, not biological calibration or long-term whole-model conservation.
