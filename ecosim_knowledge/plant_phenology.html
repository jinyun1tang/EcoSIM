Plant phenology:
clocks, seasons and life cycles

How EcoSIM moves plants from establishment through vegetative growth, reproduction, senescence and renewed growth—and how those transitions differ among crops, grasses, trees and nonvascular plants.

Phenology is a set of interacting processes

EcoSIM does not assign every species a single fixed calendar or a universal growing-degree-day threshold. A plant functional type (PFT) combines life span, seasonal response, photoperiod response, development pattern and structural traits. Branches then carry their own development dates and seasonal counters. Actual leaf area emerges from growth, resource supply and tissue loss; crossing a phenological threshold alone does not create a full canopy.

Clock 1 · Development

Nodes and stage dates

Temperature-dependent node initiation and leaf appearance advance branches toward floral initiation, anthesis and seed filling. The calendar records when each event occurred.

Clock 2 · Seasonal permission

Leaf-out and leaf-off

Hours accumulated under photoperiod, temperature or water-potential conditions open and close seasonal growth phases. Unfavorable conditions can reverse incomplete leaf-out progress.

Clock 3 · Reserve use

Mobilization and recovery

A separate physiological-time counter regulates reserve-supported growth. Seasonal eligibility and current stress also control the remobilization of existing tissues.

Where phenology sits in an hourly plant update

  1. Establishment inputSeed deposition at the first hour; activation and planting flags.
  2. PhenologyUpdateEmergence, branch development, leaf-out and leaf-off counters.
  3. Uptake and growthROOTUPTAKES, then GROWPLANTS: allocation, grain filling, reserve transfers and turnover.
  4. Re-establishmentExtraction routines, ReSeedPlants, balance closure and state return.

Within an emerged branch, developmental-stage checks precede the climate-specific counter update. Growth later in the plant call can use the updated counters. A PFT being active, a shoot being alive, and a branch having emerged are distinct conditions.

Source: PlantMod.F90 — PlantModel; PlantPhenolMod.F90 — PhenologyUpdate, Emerged_plant_Phenology.

1. Establishment and emergence are structural events

Planting dates and live-state flags determine when a configured PFT can start. Establishment draws on initialized seed/reserve pools and the associated input accounting. Planting is not equivalent to immediately supplying mature leaves or a developed root system.

TestPlantEmergence records emergence when both shoot and root geometry pass their checks:

HypocotHeight + 0.001 m > SeedDepth
and CanopyLeafArea + CanopyStemSurfArea > ZERO4LeafVar
and Root1stDepz_raxes(1,NZ) > SeedDepth + 10⁻⁸ m.

The branch calendar stores the current day of year when this condition is satisfied. Emergence is therefore coupled to biomass and structural development rather than a fixed number of days after sowing. An initialization flag can also permit the emerged-plant pathway.

Subsequent shoot branching is constrained by root turgor at the planting layer, reserves or canopy nonstructural carbon, node spacing and branch limits. Annual shoot branching is restricted to the period before floral initiation; perennials can branch beyond that stage. New branches have their own calendar and inherit part of the main branch’s leaf-out progress, so not all branches need be synchronized.

Source: set_plant_flags, TestPlantEmergence, root_shoot_branching, live_branch_phenology.

2. Vegetative and reproductive development

The developmental clock is temperature dependent

calc_leave_grow_tempf computes a nonlinear temperature response using growth temperature plus thermal offset. Node initiation and leaf appearance use separate reference rates, each multiplied by that response.

node increment = max(0, RefNodeInitRate × TFNP)
leaf-number increment = max(0, RateRefLeafAppearance × TFNP)
For annuals, multiply both by √PlantO2Stress before floral initiation, and by exp[0.025 × max(PSICanopy, −1000)] before anthesis.

These water and oxygen factors can delay annual development. The corresponding factors in this particular routine are set to one for perennials; perennial growth, seasonal counters and survival remain subject to resource and environmental constraints elsewhere.

Evergreen branches can continue node updates through their seasonal bookkeeping cycle. For other strategies, node updates stop when the leaf-off counter reaches its requirement.

The calendar contains dates, not a single continuous stage number

iPlantCalendar_brch(stage,NB,NZ) stores a day-of-year date; zero means that stage has not been recorded. The ordered stage chain advances at most one of its stages per hourly call. Let Nv be nodes added since floral initiation divided by MatureGroup_pft, and Nr be nodes added since anthesis divided by the same maturity group.

The values 2.0 and 0.667 are the current vegetative and reproductive normalization constants. These are branch-development rules, not a universal calendar duration or a guarantee that all botanical groups reproduce through literal flowers and grain.

How floral initiation is permitted

The ordinary route requires enough nodes above the branch’s baseline and either completed leaf-out hours or planting in the current year with the planting date reached and day length strictly increasing. Photoperiod then filters that route:

  • Neutral: passes the photoperiod test.
  • Short-day: PPD > PhotoPeriodSens, where PPD = max(0, CriticPhotoPeriod − DayLength). On nonshortening days, the code sets this short-day PPD to zero.
  • Long-day: the same day-length deficit must be < PhotoPeriodSens.

A separate seasonal override can initiate flowering for cold-responsive perennials (types 1 or 3) and annuals with type 0 when the canopy is above the snow and days are shortening. This route bypasses the ordinary node/leaf-out and photoperiod tests. Later stage routines also contain leaf-off-driven shortcuts; thresholds in the table are the ordinary routes, not the only possible routes.

Determinate annuals fix their final vegetative leaf number at floral initiation. Indeterminate annuals defer that assignment until stem elongation, permitting more overlap between vegetative and reproductive development.

Source: CalcPhenolEnvfactor, UpdateBranchNodeNumber, CheckBranchNodeState and InitiateBranchFloral; calc_leave_grow_tempf.

3. Four main seasonal strategies

Hours4Leafout_brch and Hours4LeafOff_brch are accumulated state variables. Their requirements are HourReq4LeafOut_brch and HourReq4LeafOff_brch. Favorable conditions do not have to be consecutive unless an explicit counter reset or reversal makes them so.

Read the executable flag tests. EnablePlantLeafOut_brch = itrue enables leaf-out accumulation; doPlantLeaveOff_brch = itrue enables the relevant leaf-off pathway. Here itrue = 1 and ifalse = 0. Some nearby inherited comments reverse that meaning.
Type 0

Evergreen and crop bookkeeping

On nondecreasing-daylength days, leaf-out hours equal the accumulated hours in the lengthening-day phase. Once the requirement is reached, leaf-off hours reset and leaf-off is enabled.

On shortening-daylength days, leaf-off hours follow the accumulated shortening-phase hours. Reaching the requirement resets leaf-out hours and enables the next leaf-out phase.

Hemisphere-specific day-of-year overrides occur at days 173 and 355, within the respective day-length branches. These counters measure elapsed simulation hours during a seasonal trend, not the number of daylight hours received.

“Evergreen” does not mean constant leaf area. Managed annual crops also use this type in the code’s intended convention.

Type 1

Cold deciduous

When leaf-out is enabled, warm hours with TCGroth ≥ TC4LeafOut add one hour. Before completion, chilling below TCChill4Seed removes one hour, bounded at zero.

The leaf-out gate accepts nondecreasing days, or shortening days while leaf-off remains incomplete. Completion—or the relevant day 173/355 override—clears leaf-off hours.

Leaf-off can be enabled after floral initiation or on shortening days shorter than 12 hours. Actual cold-hour accumulation additionally requires floral initiation and shortening days: TCGroth ≤ TC4LeafOff adds one hour.

Full leaf-off re-enables leaf-out and resets its counter when it was disabled. Enabling a flag alone does not satisfy all the accumulation gates.

Type 2

Drought deciduous

With leaf-out enabled, canopy total water potential at or above the botanical-group leaf-out threshold adds one hour. While incomplete, water potential below the leaf-off threshold subtracts 12 hours, bounded at zero.

Completion clears leaf-off hours. Floral initiation enables leaf-off. When leaf-out is disabled and leaf-off enabled, water potential below the leaf-off threshold adds one leaf-off hour.

Full leaf-off resets leaf-out hours and enables a new wet-season response. The main type-2 climate rule has no temperature or day-length gate.

Leaf-out thresholds: gymnosperms −0.5 MPa; monocots −1.5 MPa; eudicots −0.5 MPa. These vascular groups share a −2 MPa leaf-off threshold.

Type 3

Cold + drought deciduous

Leaf-out is allowed when days are nondecreasing or day length is within two hours of its annual maximum. Enabled branches add one hour if TCGroth ≥ TC4LeafOut and canopy turgor > 0.1 MPa.

Before completion, chilling or turgor below 0.1 MPa subtracts 1.5 hours, bounded at zero. Completion clears leaf-off hours.

Enabled leaf-off accumulates under shortening days or day length below 24 − annual maximum + 2 hours. Either cool temperature or low total water potential adds an hour.

Spring uses turgor; leaf-off uses total water potential. Those are different hydraulic quantities. Full leaf-off permits the next leaf-out cycle.

Requirements and thermal acclimation

The input requirements VRNLI and VRNXI supply the branch leaf-out and leaf-off thresholds. For evergreen perennials, later branches receive an additional 144 × PlantInitThermoAdaptZone × (NB−1) hours, capped at 4380 hours. Other combinations use the base requirements directly.

The thermal-acclimation setup assigns TC4LeafOut = 5 − TempOffset and TC4LeafOff = min(15, 12 − TempOffset), in °C. Therefore 5 °C and 12 °C are not universal fixed thresholds for every acclimated PFT.

Source: branch_specific_phenology, CropEvergreenPhenology, ColdDeciduousBranchPhenology, ColdDroughtDeciduPhenology; ReadPlantProperties; PFTThermalAcclimation.

Grasslike and treelike plants share the seasonal clocks; their persistence differs through structure and turnover. Woody vascular behavior requires the treelike growth form together with secondary growth. Medium roots are optional and active only for trees when lignification/secondary growth is considered; they are not an additional phenology class.

Nonvascular interpretation needs care. The bryophyte growth-form pathway omits the vascular turgor multiplier from one reserve-mobilization clock, but that does not remove all water sensitivity. Botanical group, growth form and seasonal strategy must remain compatible; in particular, the type-2 leaf-out threshold table does not cover botanical indices 0 and 1.

5. Reserve mobilization connects seasons to biomass

Reserve-supported leaf-out has its own clock

Hours2LeafOut_brch is distinct from Hours4Leafout_brch. The former accumulates physiological time used during reserve-supported development; the latter accumulates climate permission for a seasonal transition.

For the main branch, the physiological increment combines the routine’s photoperiod factor ATRPPD, a canopy growth-temperature response and a water/turgor response. The bryophyte growth-form path sets that water multiplier to one. doInitLeafOut_brch resets this clock for a new mobilization cycle.

In the eligible window, seasonal reserve carbon is oxidized at a rate proportional to the increment and GVMX × SeasonalNonstC; associated C/N/P transfers support developing shoots and roots. Current annual/perennial values of GVMX are 0.010/0.0025 and physiological-time limits are 68.96/276.9. The code operates within this window, with an annual-evergreen exception; the parameter’s inherited “initiate after” wording should not be used to reverse that condition.

Eligibility and active senescence are different flags

The current CalcPartitionCoeff logic initializes both flags each call. BegRemoblize means the branch has reached the seasonal/developmental eligibility condition. LRemob_brch means the additional condition for active remobilization is satisfied.

For grasslike and treelike forms, PSILY is −2 MPa; the bryophyte-form entry is −200 MPa. This threshold table is indexed by growth form, unlike the botanical-group table used in seasonal leaf-out/off.

HoursDoingRemob_brch increases while active, holds its value when eligible but temporarily inactive, and resets when eligibility is lost. Eligible deciduous perennials stop allocating new growth to leaves and petioles in this partition calculation; their former allocation is divided equally between stalk and reserve before normalization.

For perennials with both flags true, phenological senescence demand is proportional to leaf/sheath carbon, a turnover-pattern-specific rate, and min(1, HoursDoingRemob / 240). Maintenance deficits can supply additional remobilization demand. Other transfers into seasonal storage use eligibility itself, so “eligible but inactive” does not mean every reserve-related transfer is zero.

6. Maturity, seasonal reset and failed establishment

Maturity follows the success of grain filling

After seed number has been set, GrainFillOnBranch counts consecutive calls with carbon transfer to grain no greater than 10⁻⁹ × PlantPopuLive. Successful filling resets the counter. At 168 hours of failure, the end-of-seed-fill date is recorded. Thus physiological maturity can reflect exhaustion or stress-induced cessation of filling, rather than a fixed date after anthesis.

For non-evergreen annuals, continued failure beyond the maturity interval plus a type-dependent senescence interval forces the leaf-off counter above its requirement. The current additional intervals are 1440 hours for type 1 and 720 hours for types 2 and 3.

Perennial branch renewal

ResetBranchPhenology evaluates completed leaf-out and leaf-off for perennials and for annual types 2 and 3. In the perennial renewal path, eligible leaf-out resets the reproductive calendar entries, records a new emergence date and renews development baselines. Organ turnover depends on tissue-retention and woody-growth settings.

At completed leaf-out, the code disables the leaf-out phase, enables leaf-off and clears the preparation-for-litterfall flag. The alternative seasonal transition enables leaf-out, disables leaf-off, prepares litterfall and requests a reset of the reserve-use clock. Old tissues are routed through litter accounting; resetting stage dates does not itself erase or manufacture biomass.

The litterfall preparation path applies progressive tissue loss, with a reference loss coefficient of 0.002884 per call and a termination-counter parameter of 960. These code counters should be traced with their actual call frequency before interpreting every stored “hour” as elapsed wall-clock simulation time.

Death and reseeding are additional gates

Dead branches and roots are processed through the litterfall/death routines. Annual death can terminate both shoot and roots; perennial survival depends on the remaining structures and state flags. ReSeedPlants requires a reseeding flag and sufficient seasonal reserve carbon, then calls plant initialization and seed accounting. This is not, by itself, a population-level recruitment or seed-bank model, and its initialization accounting should not be assumed to be a closed internal seed transfer.

“False break” means a failed start to the growing season

For annual drought-deciduous and combined-strategy plants, RemoveDeadAnnual detects a stalled establishment condition: branch nonstructural carbon remains, reserve-supported development has passed its allowed window, seasonal reserve carbon remains effectively unchanged, and canopy height is negligible. The test uses the daily mean reserve state and is evaluated at the end of the day.

It must hold for 10 consecutive days; a day failing the predicate resets the counter. The reset transfers affected tissues through litter handling and reinitializes the plant’s shoot/root state. This is a failed-establishment mechanism, separate from normal seasonal leaf-off or grain maturity.

Source: GrainFillOnBranch, ResetBranchPhenology, ResetNonAnnualBranch; ResetDeadPlant and SetDeadPlant; RemoveDeadAnnual.

Key variables and units
  • iPlantCalendar_brch: stage dates, day of year; zero means not reached.
  • ShootNodeNum_brch / NumOfLeaves_brch: developmental counts; can evolve continuously between integers.
  • Hours4Leafout_brch / Hours4LeafOff_brch: climate-condition counters, accumulated hours with explicit reversals/resets.
  • Hours4LenthenPhotoPeriod_brch / Hours4ShortenPhotoPeriod_brch: simulation hours spent in the current day-length trend, not photoperiod in hours per day.
  • Hours2LeafOut_brch: physiological reserve-mobilization time, not the climate leaf-out counter.
  • HoursDoingRemob_brch: accumulated active remobilization hours while eligibility persists.
  • TCGroth_pft / TdegCCanopy_pft: growth temperature versus actual canopy temperature, °C; different routines use different fields.
  • PSICanopy_pft / PSICanopyTurg_pft: total canopy water potential versus turgor, MPa.
  • _brch quantities vary by branch and PFT; _pft quantities describe the PFT. Calendar indices are not branch numbers.

Prescribed phenology is a different execution mode

With ldo_sp_mode, PlantModel takes a prescribed-structure/uptake path instead of the prognostic phenology-and-growth sequence described above. PrescribePhenologyInterp interpolates monthly leaf and stem area and sets canopy/root structure.

Current implementation limitation: that interpolation routine contains fixed single-PFT monthly values, canopy height and population. Its non-ATS calendar fallback is January 1 with a 31-day month. Treat this as an implementation requiring case-specific inspection, not a general claim that arbitrary supplied monthly phenology is already honored in every driver.

Source: PlantModel; PrescribePhenologyInterp.
