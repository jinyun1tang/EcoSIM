The hydraulic role of mycorrhizae

EcoSIM represents mycorrhizae as an additional soil-water uptake pathway connected to the plant’s canopy water balance. Its contribution emerges from fungal geometry, hydraulic resistance, water availability, and the soil–canopy potential difference.

1. A second uptake pathway for each plant

The category index N distinguishes plant roots (ipltroot=1) from mycorrhizae (imycorrhz=2). Hydraulic routines loop over N=1,Myco_pft(NZ): a value of 1 includes roots only; 2 includes roots and mycorrhizae. NZ identifies the plant functional type and L the soil layer.

Mycorrhizae have category-specific absorptive length, radius, water volume, axis number, water potential, and radial/axial resistivities. Both categories connect to the same PSICanopy_pft(NZ). The hydraulic solver does not calculate a separate fungal-to-root interface potential or exchange resistance.

Sources: ElmIDMod.F90, category constants; UptakesMod.F90, CalcPlantHydroResistance and CanopyEnergyH2OIter_func.

Configuration matters. The prescribed-phenology uptake API explicitly sets Myco_pft=1. The description here concerns runs that enable the second category and develop nonzero mycorrhizal geometry. Merely enabling the category does not guarantee water uptake.

2. How hyphae affect hydraulic resistance

The model first derives effective absorptive geometry in UpdateRootProperty, then assembles resistance in CalcPlantHydroResistance. A category participates only when the layer has liquid water, positive soil conductivity, absorptive length, and the required primary and secondary axis counts.

Rtotal,N,L = Rsoil,N,L + Rradial,N,L + Raxial,N,L
Gplant = ΣN,L 1 / Rtotal,N,L

Fungal axial resistanceRoot2ndAxialResist_pft × effective axis length / [(r/rmax)4 × axis count]Longer individual flow paths increase resistance; more parallel axes reduce it. The fungal radius factor has a fourth-power dependence.
Host transportStalk and primary-root terms use canopy height, primary-axis count, and coarse-root lumen area.The fungal pathway still includes host-side transport costs. Mycorrhizal uptake does not bypass the plant hydraulic system.

The plant-root category uses a squared radius ratio in its secondary axial term, whereas the mycorrhizal category uses the fourth power. This distinction is explicit in the current implementation.

When RootFineFrac2Med_pvr is positive, fine transport is partitioned between a direct coarse-root connection and a connection through medium roots. Those two conductances are combined before adding the primary-root and radial terms. Medium roots are optional and only for trees; they are not a separate fungal compartment.

3. The driving potential and water limits

For each active category and layer, the trial water flux is:

qN,L = [Ψcanopy + Ψgravity − Ψsoil,L] / Rtotal,N,L

The gravitational term uses an effective uptake height of 0.8 × live canopy height. Soil potential is supplied through TotalSoilPSIMPa_vr, with the surface-elevation adjustment made during preparation.

Read the sign from the equation: RPlantRootH2OUptk_pvr(N,L,NZ) < 0 means soil water enters the plant system. A positive value means water returns to soil. Some nearby comments use a conflicting sign description.

Uptake is bounded by available micropore water, with the mineral-soil air-dry amount subtracted where applicable. Return flow is bounded by available micropore air space. Both bounds are multiplied by FracPRoot4Uptake_pvr, calculated from the category’s PopuRootMycoC_pvr divided by total root/mycorrhizal carbon across PFTs in that layer. A further canopy-water check scales down positive return flow if the plant cannot supply it.

Thus the fungal branch has access to the same modeled soil-water pool, subject to its allocation and resistances. It is not granted a separate water reservoir or an automatic drought-benefit multiplier. Adding mycorrhizae changes both conductance and allocation, so an increase in total uptake is not guaranteed.

Water potentials are in MPa and lengths/radii in metres. Flux arrays use the model’s population/grid accounting basis; do not interpret a category flux as a per-hypha rate. Convert with the actual grid area when reporting water-depth fluxes.

Source: UptakesMod.F90, PrepNutrientUptake, UpdateRootProperty, and the water-balance loop in CanopyEnergyH2OIter_func.

4. Where mycorrhizal water goes

The canopy solver sums uptake and return flow over both categories and all rooted layers. Their combined supply participates in the same iterative balance as transpiration, canopy water storage, and heat exchange. Mycorrhizal water therefore can support canopy hydration and transpiration through this shared balance.

Category-specific fluxes are also accumulated into TWaterPlantRoot2Soil_vr by ExtractsMod. Associated heat transport uses soil temperature for uptake and canopy temperature for return flow. Fungal water uptake is an accounted exchange, rather than an additional uncharged water source.

When gradients reverse, the mycorrhizal category can return water to a layer. Together with uptake elsewhere, this permits redistribution through the common plant water balance. There is no explicitly resolved fungal network connecting different host plants in these equations.

Fungal water status and biological feedback

UpdatePlantWaterVars diagnoses PSIRoot_pvr(2,L,NZ) from soil and canopy potentials weighted by hydraulic resistances. It assumes steady water status with zero root storage capacity in that pressure calculation, then calculates osmotic potential and turgor. The pressure diagnostic uses PSICanopy_pft directly; the flux calculation above uses its gravity-adjusted value.

The model nevertheless retains geometric root/hyphal water volumes for other calculations. Those volumes should not be mistaken for a separately solved fungal hydraulic capacitance. Fungal water status can affect subsequent growth; water uptake also enters nutrient-uptake calculations as a per-plant inward water flux.

Sources: ExtractsMod.F90, water/heat aggregation; UptakesMod.F90, UpdatePlantWaterVars; RootMod.F90, growth and geometry; NutUptakeMod.F90, PerPlantRootH2OUptake.

5. How to identify the fungal contribution

Inspect both categories explicitly. The clearest quantity is RPlantRootH2OUptk_pvr(imycorrhz,L,NZ). Sum max(−q,0) for gross fungal uptake and max(q,0) for gross return flow; keep those separate from the signed net exchange.

Implementation note: RootMod derives fine-root geometry from surviving biomass and lengths and refreshes all active categories and layers after growth and withdrawal, before cytokinin transport. This includes previously processed layers affected by withdrawal. The fate of cytokinin in dying tissue remains a separate accounting question. This document describes the implemented formulation, not a validation of every numerical pathway.
