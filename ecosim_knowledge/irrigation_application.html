EcoSIM process walkthrough

Irrigation application

EcoSIM builds an hourly water schedule for each cell, then sends that water through the surface precipitation pathway. Liquid nutrient concentrations, salt concentrations, pH, and the prescribed application depth share one set of values per day.

2 · Scheduled records: amounts become hourly depths

The soil-management NetCDF file selects an irrigation table through irrigf. A scheduled table contains up to 24 records, limited by nirri. This is a record-count limit for that selected table, not a limit of one event per day.

For record e, let De be its amount in mm and ne = JENe − JSTe + 1. The reader constructs:

qe,j = De / (1000 ne) when JSTe ≤ j ≤ JENe; otherwise 0.
RRIG(j,i,NY,NX) = Σe on day i qe,j   [m h⁻¹].
Σj=1…24 RRIG(j,i) Δt = Σe De / 1000   [m], with Δt = 1 h.
Multiple pulses are supported. Overlapping records add their water amounts. Every positive-water record for the same cell and day must have identical pH, the three nutrient inputs, the eight salt inputs, and application depth. Conflicts stop the model with a diagnostic. Zero-water records are ignored and cannot replace another event’s daily properties. Different dates may use different properties.

The compatibility check uses the converted numeric properties already stored for that cell and day. It also catches conflicts when another reader call has already populated the same cell/day. Blank records end the selected table; empty tables return without applying irrigation.

Source: ReadIrrigationFile, including the current pulse-reader patch.

4 · Automatic irrigation: fill a diagnosed deficit

At daily setup, eligible columns are checked within their configured seasonal window. Automatic irrigation currently creates one continuous interval with a uniform hourly rate; the reader’s support for multiple scheduled records does not make automatic irrigation generate separate pulses.

Let zL be a layer-bottom depth, zb the current topsoil upper boundary, and d the configured depletion depth. For layers whose upper boundary lies above zb + d:

fL = min[1, (zb + d − zL−1) / (zL − zL−1)]
θ*L = min[POROSL, θWP,L + C (θFC,L − θWP,L)]
V* = ΣL fL θ*L Vmic,L   ↔ TFZ
VWP = ΣL fL θWP,L Vmic,L   ↔ TWP
Vstored = ΣL fL (Vwater,L + Vice,L)   ↔ TVW

C is CIRRA_col. Here Vmic,L is VLSoilPoreMicP_vr, the bulk layer volume associated with the micropore domain, not the water-filled pore volume. The three sums above have units of m³ per cell. Stored ice participates in the current water-deficit calculation.

CriterionTrigger
Soil water, iIrrig_swc = 0Vstored < VWP + F (V* − VWP), where F = FIRRA_col.
Canopy water potential, iIrrig_cwp = 1PSICanPDailyMin_pft(1,NY,NX) < FIRRA_col(NY,NX). The current implementation uses PFT index 1.

After either trigger, the requested water volume and its hourly depth rate are:

Vrequest = max(0, V* − Vstored)   [m³ per cell]
RRIG(j,i) = Vrequest / [A (jend − jstart + 1)]   [m h⁻¹], within the interval.

A is AREA_3D(3,NU_col(NY,NX),NY,NX). Dividing by area here is essential: the hourly weather calculation multiplies by area once to recover cell-total volume. For example, a 3 m³ deficit in a 10 m² cell requests 0.3 m of water depth, not 3 m.

Automatic-input calendar convention still needs review

The source documents automatic start/end fields as DDMMHHHH and uses the last four digits as a time, but also uses those digits for leap-year tests. A source TODO records that the documented format may be a typo. This document describes that ambiguity rather than resolving it. It is separate from the scheduled-record date format DDMMYYYY.

Sources: TillageandIrrigationEvents, irrigation criterion identifiers.

5 · Surface water and heat delivery

PRECII = RRIG(j,i)   [m h⁻¹];   PRECUI = 0
Qirrig = A × PRECII = IrrigSurface_col   [m³ h⁻¹]
Qliquid = Qrain + Qirrig = PrecRainAndIrrig_col

The canopy hydrology calculation uses the combined rain-plus-irrigation supply to diagnose interception and throughfall. The surface routine partitions liquid throughfall among snow-covered, litter-covered, and exposed-soil fractions. Rain and irrigation provenance is recovered using the incoming mixture:

αirrig = Qirrig / (Qrain + Qirrig) when the denominator is positive; otherwise 0.
Qirrig→snow = αirrig Qliquid throughfall→snow
Qirrig→litter = αirrig Qliquid throughfall→litter
Qirrig→soil = αirrig Qliquid throughfall→soil

These rates are exported as Irrig2SnowSurf_col, Irrig2LitRSurf_col, and Irrig2SoilSurf_col. They describe recipient delivery after canopy partitioning, so their sum need not equal gross irrigation when canopy interception retains water.

Liquid throughfall also carries sensible heat using atmospheric air temperature in the precipitation heat terms. The basic form is cw Tair Q [MJ h⁻¹], with additional pathway scalars where present, followed by multiplication by the ground substep duration. Irrigation has no separate prescribed water-temperature input in this active path. The resulting changes in soil moisture can subsequently affect plant uptake, transpiration, and surface evaporation.

Stored depth versus active routing. WDPTH is retained, checked for same-day compatibility, and written to restart files. However, the WDPTH-based surface/subsurface branch in CalcRadiation is commented out. The active code sends all scheduled and automatic irrigation to the surface; a positive depth value alone does not implement subsurface drip delivery.

The CorrectClimate routine contains an irrigation multiplier TDIRI, but its call in the current PrepHourlyWeather is commented out. The active equations above therefore do not apply that multiplier.

Related explanations: ground-surface water and energy, canopy energy and transpiration, and surface runoff.

The reader converts elemental mass concentrations to molar concentrations: for example, CN4QX/14, CPOQX/31, and CCAQX/40. Annual chemistry initialization in StarteMod then equilibrates the input solution, including ammonium/ammonia and phosphate species. Thus, delivered species concentrations may differ from the unequilibrated input values. Gas concentration arrays have a separate column-only representation; they should not be interpreted as hourly prescribed chemistry.

For a conservative species s sent to recipient r, transport uses:

Δns,r = Qirrig→r Cs,i Δtground   [mol]
Δms,r = Δns,r Ms   [g], when a mass-based tracer pool is used.

The recipient exports are rates; dts_HeatWatTP supplies the ground substep duration in hours. Salt inputs enter snow, litter, and soil via their corresponding water exports. Nutrient pathways additionally apply the model’s band/non-band partition where required. Constant Cs,i therefore does not imply a constant hourly nutrient flux: the load follows water delivery.

For example, before speciation or reaction, two 12 mm pulses at the same prescribed elemental concentration of 14 g m⁻³ carry a total of 0.024 × 14 = 0.336 g m⁻². Changing the concentration of only the second pulse is not representable by the daily-property arrays and is rejected by the reader.

Sources: StarteMod, salt input to litter and soil, salt input to snow, nutrient deposition.

7 · Annual inputs, daily decisions, hourly application

  1. Annual preparation: reset irrigation forcing arrays and eligibility, then read the selected management records. Chemistry initialization prepares the prescribed solution.
  2. Daily setup: scheduled pulses are already in RRIG; automatic columns may construct the current day’s schedule from restored/current water state.
  3. Hourly forcing: select RRIG(J,I,NY,NX), convert depth rate to cell volume rate, and combine irrigation with rain.
  4. Ground substeps: partition water and associated heat; deliver nutrients and salts with the same recipient water rates.

For intraday continuation, RRIG_day and WDPTH_day preserve the current day’s schedule and prescribed depth. The automatic decision is not rerun partway through the day. At a midnight restart, daily setup makes the new automatic decision after loading soil state. Eligibility and prescribed chemistry are reconstructed from management inputs. Legacy intraday automatic-irrigation checkpoints without the saved schedule/depth fields are rejected; a midnight checkpoint is required.

Sources: annual forcing reset, CompleteDailySetup, restart/daily calling order, restartnc_daily_irrigation.

This walkthrough describes implemented equations and the current working-tree reader. It distinguishes gross irrigation from throughfall and infiltration, and prescribed daily chemistry from species produced by initialization. It does not establish an irrigation efficiency factor or represent sub-hour pulses: the prescribed water schedule has hourly resolution.
