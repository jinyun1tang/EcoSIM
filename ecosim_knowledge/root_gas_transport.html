Root gas transport:
from root structure to oxygen supply

EcoSIM couples atmosphere–root diffusion, soil–root exchange, and gas–water partitioning. Root geometry sets both the capacity to store gases and the conductance that supplies respiration.

Source snapshot: solphys working tree, 25 September 2026 · base commit 0d813fbd plus local fixes · primary routine: RootSoilGasExchange

Three connected transport processes

01 / AXIAL

Atmosphere ↔ root air

Gases diffuse along a primary–fine root network, with an optional medium-root branch. Flux follows the atmospheric versus internal gas concentration difference.

02 / RADIAL

Soil ↔ root interface

Diffusion and water uptake bring dissolved gases toward roots. Oxygen is consumed at an absorbing interface; other gases generally exchange with root water.

03 / PARTITIONING

Root air ↔ root water

Solubility and phase volumes determine the direction of dissolution or volatilization. Root respiration consumes O₂ and releases CO₂.

The tracked gases are CO₂, CH₄, O₂, N₂, N₂O, H₂, Ar and NH₃; dissolved NH₃ also distinguishes banded and non-banded soil zones. Within this routine, O₂ consumption and respiratory CO₂ production receive explicit biological treatment. Other gas reactions belong elsewhere in the model.

Sources: RootGasMod.F90 · RootSoilGasExchange; TracerIDMod.F90.

How root structure enters the calculation

The code distinguishes root category N, soil layer L, structural-root group NR, and plant functional type NZ. Plant roots use N=1; mycorrhizae use N=2. A structural-root group can contain multiple actual primary, medium and fine axes.

Gas storage is pooled by tracer, root category, soil layer and PFT. Primary, medium and fine-root geometry supplies transport coefficients to these shared layer pools. The gas-state arrays have no separate structural-group axis or independently evolved medium-root gas compartment.

Medium roots are optional and tree-specific. They represent intermediate lateral axes in the woody-root/lignification formulation. The growth call explicitly requires lcoarseroot, the woody-vascular predicate, positive primary length and N=ipltroot. The predicate is iPlantRootProfile_pft > 1 and iPlant2ndGrothPattern_pft == 1. llignification is a separate control; it is not directly tested at that call.

DiagRootGeometry diagnoses Vabs from root lengths, cross-sections, carbon-derived volume and turgor. With coarse-root structure enabled, it distinguishes the absorptive part of primary roots from coarse structural tissue. Consequently, total root biomass or total coarse-root volume is not automatically the volume available to store gases.

Air porosity and xylem lumen fraction are different quantities. RootPorosity_pft controls gas-filled space and gas diffusivity. The xylemPhi_* parameters and CRootLumenArea_*/MRootLumenArea_* describe liquid-water transport and cytokinin volumes. Gas transport uses the Root*TransptArea geometry, not those hydraulic lumen-area arrays.

Population basis matters. Internal population is initialized as planting density × cell area. Fine- and medium-root population lengths/counts already include that population; per-plant absorptive length does not. Some legacy array comments use area-normalized notation loosely. Follow the equations and transfers before adding another population multiplier. The name RootRaidus_rpft is also misleading: it is initialized as a dimensionless logarithmic radial-path factor, not a physical radius.

The axial diffusion network

The three RTCR* quantities are geometric conductance scalars with length units: area × axis count / path length. Gas-specific diffusivity is applied later.

G1 = max(P, n1) Ā1 / zmid   [RTCR1]
G2 = n2 πr2² / (flayer ℓ2,eff)   [RTCR2]
GM = max(P, nM) ĀM / ℓ̄M   [RTCRM]

P is live population; f_layer is FracSoiLayByPrimRoot_pvr, the rooted layer fraction; and z_mid is depth to the layer midpoint. The explicit max(P,n) count floors are part of the current primary and medium formulas. Fine-root conductance is initialized to zero unless both its count and effective length are positive.

Transport area and connection weighting

Primary and medium transport cross-sections use annular geometry, A = π(2Rδ − δ²). The tube thickness is diagnosed using FSTK, ZSTX and a minimum primary-root radius. The primary area is averaged over structural groups. The medium area now uses:

ĀM = Σr(nM,r AM,r) / ΣrnM,r

Only groups with positive medium-root structural carbon and positive medium-root counts enter that average; an empty denominator leaves area zero. Thus, adding a structural group with no medium roots does not reduce the mean area of the existing medium roots.

The local medium connection fraction is a separate geometric approximation. If medium roots are enabled and their water volume is positive, the code uses f_r = M_r / (M_r + Δz P); otherwise f_r = 0. Here M_r is population-total medium-root length in group r. The layer fraction uses the surviving fine-axis counts:

fN = Σr(n2,N,r fr) / Σrn2,N,r

Groups with fine roots but no medium roots contribute to the denominator. A group with no fine axes contributes no weight. A zero denominator gives zero. For equal fine-axis counts with local fractions 0.5 and 0, the layer fraction is 0.25.

Gdirect = G2(1−f)
Gvia M = S(G2f, GM)
Gnetwork = S(G1, Gdirect + Gvia M)   [RTCRA]

SeriesConductance implements min(a,b)/(1+min(a,b)/max(a,b)) for positive inputs. This avoids multiplying large conductances or taking reciprocals of tiny ones. A limiting fine-root segment always remains in the network.

From geometric conductance to an actual flux

Kg,step = min(Dg,T Δt τgas Gnetwork, Vair)
Δmatm→root = Kg,step (Catm − Croot,gas)

This is the emerged plant-root pathway. Because diffusivity already includes the gas substep duration, RootAtmGasConductance_rpvr behaves here as an exchange volume per substep, despite its rate-like metadata. Positive flux enters roots; negative flux leaves roots. The pore-volume cap limits the exchanged volume scale.

Conductance alone does not ensure exchange. The routine also checks activity, root-water volume and competition share. Atmospheric diffusion additionally requires emergence, positive absorptive length and the plant-root category; root gas exchange requires air-filled root volume. The primary-only fallback preserves a geometric pathway, but does not override these conditions. A wholly coarse, nonabsorptive root state may still have zero diagnosed gas storage.

Soil exchange and oxygen supply

The absorbing interface

UpdateRootProperty diagnoses a representative absorbing radius from root-water volume, porosity, per-plant absorbing length and population. It normally defines RootEffLen4Absorption_pvr = 2π × absorbing length per plant / rooted layer fraction. Special or unrooted cases use the fallback branch in that routine.

Radial geometry factor = RootEffLen4Absorption / ln[(film thickness + absorbing radius) / absorbing radius]
ΔVuptake,plant = max(−RPlantRootH2OUptk, 0) Δt / P

The soil-to-root coefficient combines an aqueous-diffusion contribution with a gas-phase contribution converted using solubility, then multiplies by the radial geometry factor. It depends on TortMicPM_vr, soil water content, FracAirFilledSoilPoreM_vr, POROQ, diffusivities and GasSolbility_vr. Increasing water-film thickness lengthens the radial diffusion path; losing soil air reduces the gas-phase contribution. The supplied water-uptake volume carries dissolved gases toward the interface.

FOXYX is a competition allocation derived primarily from this population’s oxygen demand relative to the previous ecosystem demand, with a minimum share and a biomass-based fallback. It scales aqueous diffusion and the soil O₂ volume assigned to that population. It is distinct from both root air porosity and the returned oxygen-stress limiter.

O₂ gets a demand-limited solution

Oxygen supply combines transport from soil water and from dissolved O₂ inside roots. A quadratic solution computes per-plant uptake and root-surface O₂ concentration using respiratory demand and the half-saturation parameter OXKM. Soil and root-water O₂ concentrations used by this solver are capped at atmospheric concentration × solubility. The resulting fluxes are converted to population totals.

O₂ supplied = O₂ taken directly from soil + O₂ consumed from root water
RAutoRootO2Limter = max(oscal_test, min(1, nonnegative[O₂ supplied / respiratory O₂ demand]))

RAutoRootO2Limter_rpvr is a dimensionless physiological limitation factor for each root category, soil layer and PFT. In the active gas-exchange calculation, a value of 1 means the calculated uptake meets demand; a value of 0.1 means uptake meets 10% of that demand. The test parameter oscal_test supplies a lower bound (normally zero). The inactive branch instead returns a limiter of 1 together with zero O₂ uptake.

RootO2Dmnd4Resp = 2.667 × RootRespPotent
ROOTUPTAKES (including gas exchange) → GROWPLANTS (including root growth and respiration)

Timestep relationship. In the prognostic plant update, gas transport runs before root growth. Its demand uses the previously calculated oxygen-unlimited potential respiration, RootRespPotent_pvr, carried into the uptake call. The newly diagnosed limiter then constrains physiological rates during the following growth calculation in that plant update. Growth refreshes the respiration rates used by the next uptake call.

Interpret the factor as a response to oxygen availability. It is not a remaining oxygen pool that each growth process debits. Multiplying newly calculated potential respiration by this lagged ratio does not, by itself, define a hard current-timestep oxygen budget. Reusing the factor in several physiological calculations therefore does not establish duplicate oxygen consumption. Assess oxygen and respiratory-carbon accounting by tracing the uptake, growth and gas-source updates across timesteps.

Respiratory CO₂ entering the gas routine comes from the previous timestep’s RootCO2AutorX_pvr; the routine uses −RootCO2AutorX × Δt as a positive production source. Inactive roots route that CO₂ toward soil instead of processing the active root-gas network.

Gas–water partitioning

For a gas with solubility ratio H, the code uses a solubility-scaled root-water volume H Vwater. Exchange tends toward Cwater = H Cgas. Its substep relaxation multiplier is:

DFGP = min(1, XNPD √φ Tdiff)
XNPD = 600 Δt

The proposed phase exchange is bounded by the available root gas and dissolved masses before equal-and-opposite updates are applied to the two phases. Soil phase exchange is calculated separately using soil phase volumes and DiffusivitySolutEffM_vr, with its air-filled-porosity threshold.

Mycorrhizal limitation in the current implementation. Mycorrhizae participate in soil-side oxygen uptake, but the direct atmospheric pathway and internal root gas–water exchange are restricted to N=ipltroot. The source comments explicitly identify missing oxygen supply from connected plant roots to mycorrhizae under saturated conditions. A separate mycorrhizal routing fraction does not create that missing gas-exchange pathway.

What happens during an update

  1. Prepare structure and demand. Previously updated root growth/mortality diagnostics provide counts, lengths, radii, phase volumes and routing fractions. Uptake routines derive oxygen demand from the existing potential-respiration rates and supply soil access, competition share and per-plant water uptake.
  2. Check activity. The gas routine requires positive potential respiration, root-water volume and FOXYX above their respective thresholds. Inactive cases return zero O₂ uptake and route respiratory CO₂ to soil.
  3. Calculate conductance. Combine primary, fine and optional medium paths; apply diffusivity, porosity, emergence and storage-volume constraints.
  4. Loop over soil and gas substeps. A near-zero soil-water volume skips that water substep. Otherwise, sufficiently wet/accessed soil enters the inner NPT gas loop.
  5. Solve oxygen supply and other exchanges. Determine O₂ consumption, soil–root exchange for other gases, soil gas dissolution, root phase exchange and atmosphere–root diffusion.
  6. Update masses and accumulate fluxes. Root gas and dissolved masses are updated; fluxes accumulate toward the hourly accounting. Available-pool limits and fixEXConsumpFlux constrain selected transfers.
  7. Return oxygen limitation. Total root-plus-soil O₂ uptake divided by the demand used in this gas calculation determines the physiological limiter. In prognostic mode, root growth and respiration follow uptake and prepare rates for the next update.

How to interpret structural changes

  • More fine axes: at fixed effective length and radius, lateral conductance rises linearly with count. If total fine length is fixed, changing count also changes the diagnosed mean path length.
  • Larger fine radius: the axial geometric term scales with radius squared. Radial uptake also changes through the absorbing radius and volume; this is not simply the fourth-power xylem hydraulic law.
  • Longer axial paths: primary depth and medium/fine effective lengths add diffusive resistance. Additional total length may simultaneously add absorptive surface and storage.
  • Higher porosity: raises gas storage and the φ1.33 diffusivity multiplier, while changing root-water storage and internal radial geometry. The net biological response also depends on demand, soil supply and active volume.
  • More medium routing: sends a larger fraction of fine conductance through an additional medium segment. With fixed positive segment conductances, this generally lowers the combined network conductance.
  • Saturated soil: reduces soil gas access while changing aqueous transport and phase partitioning. Internal plant-root aeration can remain important; the missing plant-to-mycorrhiza O₂ link is a distinct limitation.

The current formulation pools groups into layer means. A count-weighted area with a mean length does not, in general, equal a sum of individual group conductances Σ nA/ℓ when lengths and areas differ. The routing and area fixes improve consistency within that existing approximation.

Local pools are floored or clipped in several places. The routine computes a dtrc_err residual but does not enforce a conservation assertion there. Successful execution alone therefore does not establish exact mass conservation or scientific validity.

For a numerical experiment, inspect both supply and demand: geometry/phase volumes, RootAtmGasConductance_rpvr, soil O₂, root dissolved O₂, respiratory demand, and the final O₂ limiter. A change in root biomass can affect several of these at once.
